A B2B SEO agency helps a business that sells to other businesses get found in search. Good ones bring in qualified leads (qualified enquiries) from buyers who search for months before they speak to sales. Poor ones bring in traffic that never turns into pipeline.

This guide explains what B2B SEO is, what an agency does, how much it can cost, and how to choose one. It ends with questions to ask before you sign and mistakes to avoid.

What is B2B SEO?

B2B SEO is search engine optimization for companies that sell to other companies. The goal is to help the right people find you when they search for a problem you solve.

It differs from consumer SEO in three ways:

  • The sales cycle is long. Buyers research for weeks or months and compare several vendors.
  • Several people decide. The person who uses your product is often not the person who approves the purchase.
  • Small searches can be worth a lot. A search used by 50 people a month can bring more pipeline than one used by 5,000, if those 50 are ready to buy.

That is why B2B guides tell you to measure lead generation and pipeline, not only organic traffic and rankings.

What is a B2B SEO agency?

A B2B SEO agency is a company that plans and runs search work for business-to-business brands, from B2B SaaS companies to manufacturers. It may do the work for you, or set the plan and standards for your own team. Many also cover AI search, which is how your brand appears in tools such as ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews. The work behind it is often called generative engine optimization (GEO).

What does a B2B SEO engagement look like, step by step?

Knowing the process helps you spot an agency that is making it up as it goes. Most good engagements follow the same path. The dates below are typical, not promises.

  1. Discovery (weeks 1 to 2). The agency learns your product, your buyers and your sales process. It asks for access to Google Search Console, your analytics and, ideally, your customer system. You share your best customers and your lost deals.
  2. Audit (weeks 2 to 4). It checks your site's technical health, your current content and your competitors. The output is a written list of problems and opportunities, ranked by effort and impact.
  3. Strategy (weeks 3 to 5). It turns the audit into a plan: which buyer questions to answer first, which pages to fix, which to create, and what to stop. You agree the goals and the numbers that will show progress.
  4. Foundations (months 1 to 3). Technical fixes ship, key pages are rewritten and tracking is set up so results can be tied to leads.
  5. Content and authority (months 2 to 6 and on). New content goes live on a steady schedule. The agency earns links and mentions from trusted sites.
  6. Reporting and review (every month). You get a short report of what was done, what moved and what is next. Every quarter the plan is checked against your business goals.

If an agency cannot describe its process in this much detail, ask again. A vague answer usually means the work is vague.

How much does a B2B SEO agency cost?

There is no standard price. It depends on the size of your site, how competitive your market is, how much content you need and how much your own team does.

Published prices vary a lot. For example, one agency lists prices from $9,000 a month for its integrated B2B program, and another lists tiers from $3,000 to $68,250 a month. These are the agencies' own figures, so treat them as examples of the range, not as a benchmark.

Three things drive the price: how many pages and topics are in scope, whether the agency writes the content or advises your writers, and whether technical fixes and link work are included. Ask for a written scope and a price that matches it. Be careful with a low price that does not say what you get.

How long does B2B SEO take to work?

Google says some changes take a few hours and others take several months. It suggests waiting a few weeks before judging whether a change helped. B2B sales cycles are long too, so a page may be found in a few months but only lead to a deal later.

Be wary of any agency that promises a ranking date or a number-one position. Nobody controls Google's results.

Is SEO effective for B2B?

Yes, when it is built around how business buyers search. Buyers look for answers before they talk to a sales rep. If your pages answer their questions, you are present when they compare options. SEO also keeps working after the work is done, unlike paid ads that stop when you stop paying.

It works poorly when it chases traffic. Pages that rank for broad terms but never speak to a buyer's problem do not create pipeline.

How does B2B SEO differ by sector?

"B2B" covers very different businesses. An agency that is strong in one sector may be weak in another. Here is how the work changes:

  • Software and technology. Buyers compare products closely. Comparison pages, integration pages and clear documentation matter, and the site must be readable by search crawlers even if it relies on JavaScript. See our page on B2B SaaS.
  • Manufacturing and industrial. Buyers are engineers and specifiers. They search for specifications, applications and part numbers. Product data and technical content must be exact. See manufacturing.
  • Professional services. Trust decides the sale. Pages for each practice area and each expert, backed by clear author details, do the work. See professional services.
  • Pharma and healthcare. Claims must be accurate and reviewed. Content passes medical or legal review before it is published, so the agency must plan around your approval cycle. See pharmaceutical and life sciences.
  • Education. Buyers include institutions as well as students. Course and programme pages must be clear and easy to find. See education.

When you speak to agencies, ask which of these they have done and what they changed for their clients.

How is B2B SEO different from paid search?

Paid search, or pay-per-click advertising, puts your ad in front of buyers as soon as you pay. It stops when the budget stops. SEO takes longer to start, but the pages keep working after the work is done. Many B2B companies use both: paid search for fast tests and key terms, and SEO for lasting visibility. A good agency should be able to say how the two fit together, and what each one costs you per qualified lead.

How do you choose a B2B SEO agency?

Several guides, including ones from agencies that sell this service, give the same advice. Use these seven checks.

1. Start with your sales process

Before you look at agencies, write down how your buyers find you, who decides, and what a good lead is. Then judge each agency on how well it fits that, not on its list of services.

2. Look for B2B and niche experience

A software company, a manufacturer and a law firm all sell to businesses, but their buyers search very differently. Ask for work in your sector or a close one, and for a track record you can check. An agency that knows your buyers writes better content and needs less of your time.

3. Check who writes the content

Ask who writes, who edits and where the expertise comes from. Google asks whether content is mass-produced or outsourced to many creators so that no page gets real care. Its other questions are a good test: does it give original information, clear sourcing, and was it written or reviewed by someone who knows the topic?

4. Ask how they report results

The best reports connect organic search to leads, opportunities and revenue in your customer system. A report with only rankings and traffic hides what you most need to know.

5. Test their technical depth

B2B sites often have gated content, many languages or complex menus. Ask how the agency finds crawl and index problems, and who fixes them.

6. Ask about links and AI search

Ask how they earn links. Google's spam policies say buying or selling links for ranking purposes, and using automated programs to create links, are link spam. An agency that cannot explain its method clearly is a risk. Also ask how it tracks your brand in AI answers, because buyers now use those tools too.

7. Get a written scope, price and exit terms

You should know what is included, how much it costs, how long the contract runs and how you can leave. Make a shortlist of three or four agencies and compare them on the same questions.

Should you choose a large agency or a smaller one?

Large agencies offer many services and strong tools, but your account may be handled by junior staff. Smaller firms often give you direct access to senior people but may cover fewer services. Neither is better by itself. Ask who will actually do your work, and meet them before you sign.

Should you hire an agency or build an in-house team?

An in-house team knows your product best and works only for you. An agency brings a full set of skills at once: strategy, writing, technical work and reporting. Many companies use both. The agency sets the strategy and standards, and the in-house team supplies product knowledge and approves content.

How to compare agencies with a scorecard

After the first calls, a simple scorecard keeps the choice fair. List your criteria down the side. Score each agency from 1 to 5 in each row, and write one line of evidence for every score. Give the criteria that matter most to you more weight.

What to put in your brief to an agency

A good brief gets you good proposals. Send each agency the same one. Include:

  • Your business: what you sell, who buys and how long a sale takes.
  • Your goal: for example, 20 percent more qualified enquiries in six months.
  • Your current state: your site, your recent search results and what you have tried.
  • Your constraints: budget range, who can approve content and who can change the site.
  • What you want back: a plan, a scope, a price, and examples of similar work.

What should a monthly SEO report show?

Ask to see a sample report before you sign. A useful one has five parts:

  1. What we did: the fixes shipped and the pages published or updated.
  2. What moved: impressions, clicks and rankings for the searches that matter to buyers.
  3. What it brought: enquiries and opportunities from organic search, traced in your customer system.
  4. What we learned: what worked, what did not and why.
  5. What is next: the plan for the coming month, and anything the agency needs from you.

A report that is only a chart of traffic is not enough.

How do you work with an agency once you have chosen one?

The choice is only half the job. These habits help the work go well:

  • Name one owner on your side. Someone who can answer questions and approve work in days, not weeks.
  • Give access early. Search Console, analytics and your customer system are needed in the first week.
  • Share your sales knowledge. Your sales team hears the questions buyers ask. Pass them on.
  • Review content for accuracy. Your experts should check every claim before it is published.
  • Meet on a fixed schedule. A short monthly call beats ad hoc emails.
  • Review at 90 days. Check progress against the plan. If the foundations are not in place by then, ask why.

Questions to ask a B2B SEO agency before you hire

  • How do you do keyword research for a company like ours?
  • Can you show work for companies in our sector, and what did it change?
  • Who writes the content, and how do you get our experts' knowledge?
  • How will you connect search results to our leads and revenue?
  • How do you earn links, and will you ever buy them?
  • How do you track and improve our visibility in AI answers?
  • Who will work on our account, and how often will we meet?
  • What is included in the price, and how can we end the contract?

What should the contract cover?

Read the agreement before you sign, and ask for changes if something is unclear. Look for these points:

  • Scope: a written list of what is included and what is not.
  • Price and payment: what you pay, when, and what extra work costs.
  • Term and exit: how long it runs and how you can leave. Check any minimum term.
  • Ownership: you should own the content, the accounts and the data produced for you.
  • Access: the agency should use accounts in your name, so you keep everything if you leave.
  • Reporting: what you will receive and how often.
  • Methods: a promise to follow Google's guidelines and not to buy links.

What are the warning signs of a poor B2B SEO agency?

  • It guarantees a ranking or a date for page one.
  • It cannot explain how it earns links.
  • It sends the same plan to every client.
  • It will not show a sample report or name a reference.
  • It reports traffic only.
  • It asks you to sign a long contract before it has seen your site.
  • It refuses to use accounts in your name.
  • It sells you a long list of keywords instead of a plan.

What mistakes should you avoid?

  • Choosing on price alone. A cheap plan that delivers nothing is the most expensive one.
  • Trusting a ranked list from an agency. Many "best agency" lists are written by agencies that rank themselves first. Use them to build a shortlist, then check references and case studies yourself.
  • Accepting a ranking guarantee. Nobody can promise a number-one position.
  • Judging by traffic. Ask for enquiries and pipeline.
  • Skipping the questions above. The answers tell you more than a sales deck.

Wrapping up

Choose a B2B SEO agency that understands your buyers, shows who does the work, reports in leads and revenue, and gives you a clear written scope. Take your time: a wrong choice costs months.
