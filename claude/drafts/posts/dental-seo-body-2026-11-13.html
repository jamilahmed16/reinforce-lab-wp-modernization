Dental SEO is the work of making your dental practice easy to find in search. When someone types "dentist near me" or "emergency dentist" into Google, you want your practice to show up, look trustworthy and get the call.

This guide explains what dental SEO is, why it matters, and how to do it step by step. It covers Google Business Profile, local search, service pages, reviews, technical basics, cost and the mistakes that cost practices patients.

What is dental SEO?

Dental SEO (search engine optimization for dentists) is the set of steps that help a dental practice rank in Google, Google Maps and AI search tools when patients look for care. It covers your website, your Google listing, your reviews and your content.

It differs from general SEO in three ways:

  • It is local. Most patients choose a dentist close to home or work.
  • The decision is personal. People are choosing someone to look after their health, so trust and reviews carry great weight.
  • Searches are urgent or planned. Some patients need help today. Others compare practices for weeks before they book a larger treatment.

Why is SEO important for dentists?

Patients search before they call. If your practice is not in the results, a competitor gets the booking. SEO matters because:

  • It reaches people who are ready to book. A search for "dentist accepting new patients" shows clear intent.
  • It builds steady, lasting traffic. Unlike ads, a ranked page keeps working after you stop paying for it.
  • It supports trust. Good reviews, clear pages and helpful answers show that your team is real and careful.
  • It lowers your cost per new patient over time. Ads stop when the budget stops. Good pages keep bringing visits.

SEO is not instant. Google says changes can take from a few hours to several months to show, so treat it as a long-term job.

Step 1: Set up and improve your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is the listing that appears in Google Maps and in the local results box. For many practices it brings more calls than the website does.

Check each of these:

  • Name, address and phone (NAP). Use the exact same details everywhere online.
  • Categories. Choose "Dentist" as the main category. Add others that match your services, such as cosmetic dentist or emergency dental service, only if they are true.
  • Opening hours. Keep them correct, including holidays.
  • Services. List the treatments you offer in plain words.
  • Photos. Add real photos of the reception, treatment rooms and team, with permission.
  • Booking link. Link to your online booking page or phone number.
  • Posts and updates. Share offers, news and tips from time to time.

Do not put keywords in the business name. Use your real practice name. Google's rules ask for the name you use in the real world, and listings with added keywords can be suspended.

Step 2: Get and manage patient reviews

Reviews affect both your ranking in local results and a patient's choice to book. Make it easy and honest.

  • Ask after a good visit. Send a short message with a direct link to your Google review page.
  • Ask everyone, not only happy patients. Selective asking can break platform rules.
  • Reply to every review. Thank people. Keep replies short.
  • Protect privacy. Never confirm that a reviewer is a patient or share any health detail in a public reply. Say something general, such as "Please contact the practice so we can help."
  • Do not pay for reviews or write your own. Review platforms can remove them, and some countries treat fake reviews as unfair practice.

An example reply to a complaint: "Thank you for telling us. We are sorry your visit did not go as you hoped. Please call the practice manager so we can talk it through." It is polite, short and shares no private details.

Step 3: Build a clear website

Your website is where patients check that you are the right fit. It should load fast, work well on a phone and make the next step obvious.

Pages you need

  • Home page. Who you are, where you are and how to book.
  • One page for each treatment. Examples: check-ups, hygiene, fillings, whitening, implants, braces or aligners, emergency care.
  • One page for each location, if you have more than one.
  • Team page. Names, qualifications and a short note on each person.
  • Contact and booking page. Address, map, phone, opening hours and a form.
  • Prices or "how we price" page, if you can share it.

What a good treatment page includes

  1. A clear title with the treatment and the place, such as "Teeth whitening in Leeds."
  2. A plain explanation of what the treatment is and who it suits.
  3. How the visit works, step by step.
  4. How long it takes and what care afterwards looks like.
  5. The dentist who does it, with qualifications.
  6. Honest information on cost or how to get a quote.
  7. Answers to common questions.
  8. A button to book.

Write each treatment page for that treatment alone. One page that lists every service in a short line will rarely rank for any of them.

Step 5: Optimize each page (on-page SEO)

On-page SEO means making each page clear to patients and to search engines.

  • Title tag. Include the treatment and the place. Keep it under about 60 characters so it is not cut off.
  • Meta description. Write one clear sentence on what the page offers and why to book.
  • Headings. One main heading, then clear subheadings that match patient questions.
  • Short paragraphs and plain words. Patients may be anxious. Explain simply.
  • Internal links. Link from related pages, for example from a blog on gum health to your hygiene page.
  • Image alt text. Describe each image in a few words for screen readers and search.
  • Schema markup. Structured data tells search engines your business type, address, hours and reviews. Add Dentist or LocalBusiness data and FAQ data where it fits.

Step 6: Create local landing pages

If you serve several areas or have more than one site, make a page for each place you really serve. Each page needs its own content: the address, directions, parking, local transport, the team at that site and local patient questions.

Do not copy one page and swap the town name. Search engines see through it, and patients do too. If you cannot write something real for a place, do not make a page for it.

Step 7: Write helpful content

Patients search with questions. Articles that answer them bring visits, build trust and support your treatment pages.

Ideas that fit most practices:

  • "How often should I see a dentist?"
  • "What happens at a first dental visit?"
  • "Dental implants vs bridges: how to choose"
  • "What to do in a dental emergency"
  • "How to look after your teeth after whitening"
  • "Is teeth straightening right for adults?"

Have a dentist check every health claim before you publish it. Google asks for first-hand experience and expertise on pages that touch health. Name the author, add their qualifications and show the date you last reviewed the page. Do not promise results or make claims you cannot support.

Step 9: Build local links and mentions

Links from other sites tell search engines you are a real, trusted local business. Good, honest sources include:

  • Your professional body or dental association listing
  • Local business directories and the chamber of commerce
  • Local news, schools and charities you support
  • Sponsorship of local sports teams or events, with a link on their site
  • Local health or lifestyle bloggers who review your practice

Do not buy links. Google's spam policies list buying links to influence rankings as link spam.

Step 10: Track what matters

Track results that tie to patients, not only rankings.

  • Calls and booking requests from Google Business Profile and your site
  • Direction requests from Maps
  • Form fills and online bookings from search, tracked with Google Analytics
  • Impressions and clicks in Search Console
  • New reviews and your average rating

Ask every new patient, "How did you find us?" and record the answer. It costs nothing and shows what is working.

This example is illustrative. It shows how the steps fit together for a practice with one site and two dentists.

Dental SEO for AI search

Patients also ask AI tools and Google's AI answers questions such as "how do I find a good emergency dentist?" These tools draw on pages that give clear, direct answers and on trusted local sources.

You can help in simple ways:

  • Answer questions directly. Put a short, plain answer at the top of each FAQ.
  • Keep facts consistent. Your hours, address and services should match on your site, your profile and your listings.
  • Show who you are. A named dentist with qualifications is a stronger signal than an anonymous page.
  • Earn real reviews and mentions. Independent sources that describe your practice add weight.
  • Use clear structure. Short headings, lists and tables are easy to read for people and for machines.

There is no trick that guarantees an AI tool will name your practice. The steps above are the same ones that help in normal search.

Dental SEO for more than one location

Practice groups need extra care. Each site should have its own Google Business Profile, its own location page and its own reviews. Check that each profile uses the right phone number and opening hours, and that each location page covers its own team and area.

Keep a simple tracker with one row per site: profile status, number of reviews, average rating, location page status and monthly calls. This shows quickly which site needs work.

Emergency and same-day searches

Emergency searches are urgent and often come from a phone. Patients want a number to call and to know you are open. Make these easy to find:

  • A click-to-call number at the top of every page
  • A page for emergency dental care that explains what to do and when to call
  • Opening hours that match your profile, with out-of-hours instructions
  • A line that says whether you take new patients for urgent visits

Write the page in calm, simple words. Say that anyone with severe swelling, heavy bleeding or trouble breathing should seek urgent medical help and not wait for an appointment.

Do video and social media help dental SEO?

They can help, in a supporting role. Social posts do not directly raise your rankings, but they bring people to your site and give patients a feel for your team before they book.

  • Short videos that explain a treatment, a first visit or a dental tip can answer questions and build trust. Host them on YouTube and add them to the matching page.
  • Team introductions help nervous patients. A 30-second hello from the dentist can lower the barrier to booking.
  • Social profiles on Facebook or Instagram often rank for your practice name. Keep the name, address, phone and link the same as everywhere else.

Always get written consent before you show a patient's face, voice or treatment on video or social media. Check your regulator's rules on advertising and patient privacy.

If you hire, ask for a written scope, a plain price, who writes the content, how reports will tie to calls and bookings, and how you can leave the contract. Be careful with anyone who promises a ranking date or the number-one spot. Nobody controls Google. Also check the advertising rules for your country and your regulator. Some rules limit what a dental practice may say about results, offers and before-and-after photos.

How much does dental SEO cost?

There is no standard price. It depends on the number of locations, how competitive your area is, how much content you need and who does the work. Published agency prices vary widely and change often, so get written quotes from several providers and compare the scope, not only the price.

The cost has three parts:

  • People: your staff time, a freelancer or an agency fee.
  • Tools: keyword, ranking and reporting software.
  • Content and links: photos, writing, and any local sponsorship.

How long does dental SEO take?

Google says some changes show in hours and others take months. For a local practice, fixing a profile and collecting reviews can lift calls in weeks. Building pages that rank for treatment searches usually takes several months. Plan to work for at least six months before you judge the result.

Common dental SEO mistakes

  • One page for every treatment. Give each main treatment its own page.
  • Inconsistent NAP. A different phone number or address on different sites confuses patients and search engines.
  • No review plan. Reviews arrive by habit, not by chance.
  • Copied location pages. Each page needs real local content.
  • Ignoring mobile. Patients often search from a phone.
  • Keyword-stuffed names and text. It reads badly and can break platform rules.
  • Chasing rankings over bookings. Track calls and appointments.
  • Sharing private details. Never discuss a patient's care in a public reply.

A quick checklist

  • Google Business Profile is complete, with the right category and hours
  • NAP is the same everywhere
  • A steady plan to ask for reviews, and replies to every review
  • A page for each main treatment
  • Each page has a clear title, headings, FAQs and a booking button
  • Pages load fast and work on a phone
  • Dentist-checked articles that answer patient questions
  • A few honest local links
  • Calls, bookings and Search Console tracked every month

Final thoughts

Dental SEO works when it is honest, local and steady. Start with your Google Business Profile and reviews, build clear treatment pages, and answer patient questions in plain words. Track calls and bookings, and improve one page at a time. Over time, that turns searches into new patients.
