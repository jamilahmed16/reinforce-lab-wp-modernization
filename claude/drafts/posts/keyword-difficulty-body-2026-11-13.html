Keyword difficulty (KD) is a score that estimates how hard it is to rank on the first page of Google for a search term. A low score means it is easier. A high score means you face strong competition.

This guide explains what keyword difficulty is, how tools work it out, what a "good" score is for your site, and how to check a keyword yourself in 10 minutes. It also shows how to turn the score into a content plan.

What is keyword difficulty in SEO?

Keyword difficulty is a number, usually from 0 to 100, that tells you how competitive a search term is. It is also called keyword competition or SEO difficulty. The higher the number, the harder it is to reach page one.

Two facts matter before you use it:

  • Google does not publish it. KD is not a Google metric. SEO tools such as Semrush, Ahrefs and Moz make their own estimate.
  • It is an estimate. Every tool uses its own method, so the same keyword can get different scores in different tools.

Use KD to compare keywords inside one tool. Do not compare a Semrush score with an Ahrefs score.

Why does keyword difficulty matter?

Time and money for content are limited. KD helps you choose where to spend them.

  • It saves effort. A new site that targets a very hard keyword can wait a year and still rank nowhere.
  • It finds quick wins. Easier keywords can bring visits sooner, which gives you data and links to build on.
  • It sets honest goals. It tells you whether you need one good page or a page plus many links.
  • It balances your plan. You can mix easy keywords for early traffic with harder ones for later.

The numbers show why the choice matters. Ahrefs studied about 14 billion pages in its Content Explorer database in 2023. It estimated that 96.55% of them got no traffic from Google, and 1.94% got between one and ten visits a month. Ahrefs says its traffic numbers are estimates and that the sample leans toward the "quality side of the web", so the real figure may differ. The point still holds: most pages never reach the searchers they were written for.

Position matters too. Backlinko analyzed about 4 million Google results and found that the first result had an average click rate of 27.6%, that the top three results took 54.4% of clicks, and that only 0.63% of searchers clicked anything on page two. That study came before AI answers, so treat the exact figures as a historical baseline. A hard keyword where you land on page two earns almost nothing, which is why picking a keyword you can reach comes first.

How is keyword difficulty calculated?

Each tool looks at the pages that rank in the top 10 for the keyword and scores how strong those pages are. The tools publish more detail than many people think. Here are two of them, as the vendors describe their own scores:

ToolScaleWhat goes into the score
Ahrefs0 to 100How many websites link to each of the top 10 pages. It does not use your own site's authority.
Semrush0 to 100%A weighted formula. The median number of referring domains to the ranking pages carries 41.22% of the weight. The median Authority Score of the ranking domains carries 16.99%. Search volume carries 9.47%. The ratio of dofollow to nofollow links carries 9.17%. Search result features such as featured snippets (4.74%), local packs (3.39%) and "People also ask" boxes (1.36%) carry the rest, and keywords with no features score lower.

Semrush says it built its formula by studying the movement of results for 120,000 keywords and more than 100 parameters. It uses the median of the top 10 so that one very strong page does not inflate the score.

Both tools lean mostly on links. That is why a keyword can look easy to a tool when the content on page one is weak, or hard when the content is good but old. Scores also differ between tools, because each one uses its own formula and its own link data.

Other inputWhat it tells you
Content quality and fitWhether the top pages match what searchers want and cover the topic well. Tools score this little or not at all, so check it by hand.
Search intentWhether results are guides, product pages or tools. Your page must fit the same type.

What do the score ranges mean?

Each tool labels its own scale. Semrush publishes six levels. Your own site's strength changes how you read them.

Semrush KDLabelWhat Semrush says it takes
0 to 14Very easyThe best chance to rank a new page with the least effort.
15 to 29EasySome competition, but achievable even for a new domain with quality content that fits the intent.
30 to 49PossibleQuality, well-structured, optimized content.
50 to 69DifficultQuality content plus some quality backlinks.
70 to 84HardA lot of effort to win better backlinks to support strong content.
85 to 100Very hardContent, link building, on-page work and promotion, all at a high level.

Ahrefs describes its scale in terms of links. A KD of 0 to 5 means the top pages barely have any backlinks. Around KD 50, the top pages have a couple of hundred backlinks. At KD 90 and above, they have thousands. In one example on its page, a KD of 6 is labeled easy, with about 7 referring domains needed to reach the top 10.

A new site should start in the easy bands and move up as it earns links and trust. An older site with many links can aim higher.

What is a good keyword difficulty score to target?

There is no single good score. It depends on your site's strength. Here is a simple way to choose:

  1. Find a keyword you already rank for on page two or three. Note its KD.
  2. Look at the KD of the keywords you rank for on page one. The highest of these is your "reach."
  3. Aim for keywords at or a little above that reach. Mix in some easier ones for quick wins.

If you have no rankings yet, start at 0 to 30 and move up as your pages gain traffic and links.

Keyword difficulty vs search volume

Search volume is how many times a month people search for a term. KD is how hard it is to rank. You need both.

MixWhat to do
High volume, low KDBest target. Check intent, then build a strong page.
High volume, high KDA long-term goal. Build supporting pages and links first.
Low volume, low KDA quick win, especially if the searcher is ready to buy.
Low volume, high KDUsually skip it.

For a business that sells to other businesses, a small search can be worth a lot. A term searched 50 times a month by buyers may beat one searched 5,000 times a month by students. Volume and KD do not show this. Look at intent too.

How to check keyword difficulty step by step

You can read the tool's score in seconds. A good check takes about 10 minutes more. Follow these steps.

Step 1: Get the tool's score

Enter the keyword in a tool such as Semrush, Ahrefs, Moz or a free checker. Write down the KD and monthly volume.

Step 2: Read the first page of results

Search the keyword in Google. Look at the top 10 results. Ask:

  • Are they big brands or small sites?
  • Are they guides, product pages, lists or tools?
  • Is there an AI answer, video or map above them?

Step 3: Match the intent

Search intent is the reason behind the search. If every result is a how-to guide, a sales page will not rank. Plan a page of the same type, then make it better.

Step 4: Look for weak spots

Signs that a keyword may be easier than its score:

  • Old pages that have not been updated in years
  • Thin pages that skim the topic
  • Small or little-known sites in the top 10
  • Forum threads or social posts ranking on page one

Step 5: Count the links needed

Open the top few pages in a link tool. See how many sites link to each. If the top pages have only a handful of links, you may need few. If they have hundreds, plan for outreach.

Step 6: Decide

Choose the keyword, or pick a more specific version of it (a long-tail keyword). A long-tail keyword is a longer, narrower phrase, such as "keyword difficulty for new websites" instead of "keyword difficulty." These usually have lower scores and clearer intent.

This is the usual pattern: win the easy keywords, build trust, then move up.

Strategies for each difficulty level

Low difficulty (0 to 30)

Write a complete, accurate page that fits the intent. Add a clear title and headings. Link to it from your other pages. Often this is enough.

Medium difficulty (31 to 60)

You need a page that is clearly better than what ranks: more detail, fresh examples, original images or data. Then earn some links with outreach, guest articles or a useful free tool.

High difficulty (61 to 100)

Treat it as a project. Build a group of pages around the topic, a topic cluster, so the site shows depth. Earn strong links over time. Do not expect fast results.

How accurate is keyword difficulty?

It is a useful guide, not a promise. Reasons it can mislead you:

  • Tools disagree. Each uses its own formula and data.
  • It leans on links. A page with few links but excellent content can still win.
  • It ignores your site. Ahrefs says its score shows how hard it is for the average site and does not account for your own site's authority. A site with strong authority in a topic ranks more easily than the score suggests. Semrush offers a separate personal keyword difficulty score for this reason.
  • Results change. Rankings and scores move, so recheck before you commit.
  • Intent can matter more. A page that does not fit what people want will fail at any score.
  • Results are personal. Location and search history can change what you see.

Use KD to shortlist. Use your own reading of the results to decide.

Which tools show keyword difficulty?

Most keyword tools show a KD score next to each term. They differ in name, scale and method, so pick one and stay with it when you compare keywords.

Type of toolExamplesGood to know
Full SEO suitesSemrush, Ahrefs, Moz, SimilarwebPaid. They show KD, volume, the top pages and link counts in one place.
Free checkersFree tiers and keyword tools from the same brands, plus other small toolsLimited searches a day, and fewer details. Fine for a first look.
Google Search ConsoleGoogle's free tool for site ownersIt does not show KD. It shows the searches you already get impressions for, which helps you spot keywords you almost rank for.

Names also differ. Some tools call the score "keyword difficulty," some "keyword competition," and some "SEO difficulty." Check the tool's help page to see what it counts.

Does Google use keyword difficulty?

No. Keyword difficulty is not a Google metric. Semrush says so on its own page: it is an estimate created by SEO tools to help you compare keywords. Google does not publish a difficulty score, and its ranking systems do not use one. The score only summarizes what a tool can see about the pages that rank today.

Why do Ahrefs and Semrush give different scores?

They measure different things. Ahrefs looks at referring domains to the top 10 pages. Its scale is not linear: each value on it matches an estimated number of referring domains a page needs to reach page one, so the jump from KD 60 to KD 70 costs far more links than the jump from 10 to 20. Semrush blends links with domain authority, link type, search volume and search result features, and it uses medians.

Search Engine Land's guide to keyword difficulty adds that Moz uses its own authority scores together with expected click-through rates. The result is that one keyword can score 20 in one tool and 60 in another, and each can be reasonable for what it measures. Pick one tool and compare keywords inside it. Do not compare a score from one tool with a score from another.

Can you still rank for a high-difficulty keyword?

Yes, but plan for it. Ahrefs says its score is meant to help you estimate the resources needed, not to discourage you, and that if a keyword is vital to your business you may still pursue it. A hard keyword needs a stronger page, more links and more time. It can also hide weak spots. Ahrefs notes that an average of referring domains can mask problems in the results, such as old content, brands with low authority or thin pages, and these are openings you can use.

A practical approach is to win the easier keywords around the topic first. They build links, traffic and proof of expertise. Then take on the hard head term from a stronger position. There is no standard time frame. We found no reliable public study on how long a high-difficulty keyword takes, so we do not quote one. Track your own rankings and learn your own pace.

Keyword difficulty, keyword competition and paid competition

These terms get mixed up. They are not the same.

  • Keyword difficulty: how hard it is to rank in the normal (organic) results.
  • Paid competition: how many advertisers bid on the term in Google Ads. A term can have many bidders and still be easy to rank for.
  • Cost per click (CPC): what advertisers pay for one click. A high CPC often means the term has commercial value.

Use all three together. A keyword with low KD and a high CPC is often a strong target, because it is winnable and buyers search for it.

What is personal keyword difficulty?

The score in a tool does not know your site. Your own chance depends on:

  • Your site's links and trust. Strong sites can rank for harder terms.
  • Your topic fit. A site that covers a topic in depth ranks more easily for related terms.
  • Your content. A page with real examples, clear answers and good structure beats a thin page.
  • Your resources. Link building takes time and budget.

A simple way to adjust: compare your site's strength to the sites in the top 10. If your site looks like the weaker ones, you have a fair chance. If every result is a giant brand, the real difficulty for you is higher than the score.

How to build a keyword plan from KD

Use difficulty to sort your list into waves. This turns a long list of keywords into a calendar.

  1. Collect 50 to 100 keywords for one topic, from your tool, Search Console, customer questions and competitor pages.
  2. Add volume and KD for each in a spreadsheet.
  3. Mark the intent as learn, compare or buy.
  4. Group by topic. Put similar keywords in one group. One page can often serve a whole group.
  5. Sort into waves. Wave 1 is easy and relevant. Wave 2 is medium. Wave 3 is hard but valuable.
  6. Write wave 1 first, link the pages together, then review results after two to three months.

This reading is a rough guide, not a rule, but it is more useful than the score alone because it shows the target in real numbers.

Plan how you will earn those links: a useful tool or template, original data from your own work, expert quotes, or guest articles on relevant sites. Do not buy links. Google's spam policies treat buying links to pass ranking signals as link spam.

How to improve your odds on a hard keyword

You cannot change the score, but you can change your chances.

  • Cover the topic fully. Answer the main question, then the follow-up questions people ask next.
  • Add what others lack. A worked example, a checklist, a template, a simple diagram or your own data.
  • Show your experience. Say who wrote the page and why they know the topic. Google's guidance on helpful content asks for first-hand experience and expertise where it fits.
  • Build topic depth. Publish related pages and link them to each other so the site looks like a reliable source on the topic.
  • Fix the basics. Fast loading, a clear title, short paragraphs and working mobile layout.
  • Refresh it. Update examples and facts every few months.

Keyword difficulty for local and B2B businesses

The score works differently outside big consumer topics.

  • Local searches such as "dentist in Leeds" show a map and local listings. Your Google Business Profile, reviews and location pages matter more than the KD number.
  • B2B searches often have low volume and low KD, but high value. A page that answers a buyer's detailed question can bring in a few strong enquiries a month.
  • New products or topics may have no volume data. Use customer questions and sales calls to find terms, and test with one page.

Keyword difficulty and AI search

Many searches now show an AI answer above the normal results. This changes what a ranking is worth. A page may rank well and still get fewer clicks if the AI answer satisfies the searcher.

Two studies show the size of the effect, with limits. The Pew Research Center tracked 900 US adults in March 2025. Users clicked a regular result in 8% of visits to pages with an AI summary and 15% of visits to pages without one. GrowthSRC studied more than 200,000 keywords on 30 or more sites and reported that the click rate for position 1 fell from 28% to 19% between 2024 and 2025. That is a sample of client sites, not all of Google, so use it as a direction and not as a rule.

Two things help. First, aim for questions where people want more than a short answer, such as comparisons, checklists and worked examples. Second, write clear, direct answers near the top of your page. These can be picked up by AI tools as well as by Google's own features.

How to check keyword difficulty for free

You do not need a paid tool to get a useful first answer. Try this:

  1. Use a free checker. Many keyword tools give a few free searches a day with a KD score and volume.
  2. Read the results page. Count the big brands in the top 10. Seven or more large sites is a hard page.
  3. Check the page titles. If few titles use your exact phrase, you may have room to win with a clear, matching title.
  4. Use "People also ask" and related searches. They show the follow-up questions you can answer to beat thinner pages.
  5. Use Search Console. If your site already shows impressions for a phrase at positions 8 to 20, a small update may lift it. This is the cheapest win, because Google already links the page to the topic.

The free route gives you a rough read in 10 minutes. A paid tool saves time when you have a long list.

How often should you recheck keyword difficulty?

Scores and results change. Recheck your target keywords every three to six months, and before you update an older page. If new big brands have entered the results, or the page types have changed, adjust the plan.

Also track your own rankings after you publish. If a page sits on page two for several months, look at the top results again. You may need more depth, better links or a closer match to intent. Keyword difficulty is where you begin, not the whole job.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing only by volume. A big number means big competition.
  • Trusting one score. Check the real results page.
  • Ignoring intent. The wrong page type will not rank.
  • Chasing only easy keywords. Some easy terms have no value for your business.
  • Never rechecking. Review your targets every few months.

A quick checklist

  • Note the KD and monthly volume in one tool.
  • Read the top 10 results and note the page types.
  • Check how strong the ranking sites are.
  • Look for old, thin or weak pages.
  • Count the links the top pages have.
  • Pick a long-tail version if the main term is too hard.
  • Match the intent, then write a better page.

Final thoughts

Keyword difficulty will not tell you whether you will rank. It tells you how steep the climb looks. Pair it with search volume, intent and a look at the real results, and you can choose keywords that fit your site today. Start with the ones you can win, and build from there.
