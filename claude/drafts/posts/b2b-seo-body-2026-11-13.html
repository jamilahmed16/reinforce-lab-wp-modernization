B2B SEO is search engine optimization for companies that sell to other companies. The aim is simple: when a business buyer searches for a problem you solve, your pages appear, answer the question and lead to an enquiry.

This guide explains what B2B SEO is, how it differs from consumer SEO, and how to build a B2B SEO strategy in eight steps. It also covers keywords, content, links, AI search, measurement and common mistakes. If you are thinking of hiring help, read our guide to B2B SEO agencies too.

What is B2B SEO?

B2B SEO is the practice of improving a business-to-business website so it ranks in Google and appears in AI answers for the searches your buyers make. That covers your pages, your technical setup, your links from other sites and the content that answers buyer questions.

The goal is not traffic for its own sake. It is qualified enquiries: people who match your customer profile and are likely to talk to sales.

How is B2B SEO different from B2C SEO?

B2B SEOB2C SEO
Who buysSeveral people in a company: users, managers, finance, leadersUsually one person or household

The biggest difference is the buying group. A page may be read by the person who will use your product, by the manager who approves it and by the person who checks the contract. Each asks different questions, so your content must cover all three.

Why is SEO important for B2B companies?

  • Buyers research before they talk to you. They read, compare and shortlist on their own. If you are not in the results, you are not on the shortlist.
  • It reaches people at the right moment. A search is a stated need, unlike an ad that interrupts.
  • It builds assets. A strong page keeps working for months or years.
  • It supports sales. Good content answers questions your team gets on every call.
  • It supports AI search visibility. The same clear, trusted pages are what AI tools pull from when buyers ask for options.

SEO is slow. Google says changes can take from a few hours to several months to show. Plan for a program of six to twelve months, not a quick campaign.

B2B SEO strategy: 8 steps

Step 1: Define your buyers

List the people involved in a purchase. For each, write their job, their main problem and the questions they ask before they buy. Use sales calls, support tickets and customer interviews, not guesses.

RoleWhat they care aboutQuestions they search
UserWill it work for my tasks?how to do [task], [product type] features
ManagerWill it save time and money?best [product type] for [team size], [product] vs [competitor]
Finance or procurementWhat does it cost and what are the terms?[product] pricing, [product] contract length
LeaderIs it safe and will it grow with us?[product] security, [product] case study

Step 2: Set goals tied to the business

Choose goals that sales and finance care about. Examples: demo requests from organic search, enquiries from a named industry, or share of leads from search. Track traffic as a means, not the goal.

Step 3: Run an audit

An SEO audit shows where you stand. Check:

  • Technical health. Can search engines crawl and index your key pages? Is the site fast and secure?
  • Content. Which pages bring visits and enquiries? Which are thin or out of date?
  • Keywords. What do you already rank for, and where are the gaps?
  • Links. Who links to you, and how does that compare with your rivals?
  • Competitors. Who ranks for your key searches?

Step 4: Do keyword research by buyer stage

Map keywords to the path a buyer takes. Include small searches. In B2B, a search made 30 times a month by the right buyers can beat one made 3,000 times a month by the wrong audience.

StageExample searchPage to build
Problem awarewhy do invoices get paid lateA guide that explains the causes and fixes
Solution awareinvoice automation software for agenciesA solution page for that industry
Comparing[your product] vs [competitor]A fair comparison page
Ready to buy[your product] pricingA pricing or "how we price" page
Needs proofinvoice automation case studyA case study with real results

The examples above are illustrative. Use your own market terms and check volume and difficulty in a keyword tool. Read the top results to make sure your page matches the type of page that ranks.

Step 5: Build the right pages

Most B2B sites need these page types:

  • Product or service pages. One for each offer, written for the buyer's problem.
  • Industry pages. One for each sector you serve, with its own examples.
  • Use-case pages. Show how the product solves a specific task.
  • Guides and explainers. Answer questions at the top of the funnel.
  • Comparison and alternative pages. Be fair and factual.
  • Case studies. Real clients, real results, with their permission.
  • Pricing or how we price. Buyers look for it, so say something useful.

Write each page to answer one main question completely. Add real examples, numbers you can source, and names of the experts behind the page.

Step 6: Show experience, expertise and trust (E-E-A-T)

Google's guidance on helpful content asks whether a page shows first-hand experience, expertise, authority and trust. For B2B this means:

  • Named authors with real job titles and short bios
  • Examples from your own work and customers
  • Clear sources for facts and statistics
  • A visible "last updated" date
  • Reviews, client logos you have permission to show, and security or compliance details where they matter

Step 7: Fix technical SEO and structure

  • Crawl and indexing. Make sure important pages can be found and indexed. Use a sitemap and check Search Console for errors.
  • Speed and mobile. Buyers often read on a phone between meetings.
  • Site structure. Group pages by topic and link them so buyers and search engines can follow the path.
  • Titles and headings. Use the words buyers use, near the start.
  • Schema markup. Add structured data for your organization, articles, FAQs and products where it applies.
  • International setup. If you sell in several countries, use the right language and region markup.

Step 8: Earn links and mentions

Links from trusted sites help your pages rank. In B2B, good sources are industry bodies, trade press, partner and customer sites, podcasts, conference pages and respected blogs. Ways to earn them:

  • Publish original data from your own work
  • Offer expert quotes to journalists and writers
  • Build a free tool or template people want to share
  • Write guest articles for relevant sites
  • Collect links from partners and clients who already know you

Do not buy links. Google's spam policies list buying or selling links for ranking purposes as link spam.

How do you build topic clusters in B2B?

A topic cluster is a group of pages on one subject. One main page covers the topic broadly, and several support pages cover parts of it in detail. All of them link to each other. This shows search engines and buyers that you know the subject well.

For example, a company that sells payment software for marketplaces could build a cluster like this:

  • Main page: marketplace payments, a complete guide
  • Support pages: how split payments work, marketplace payout schedules, fraud checks for marketplaces, how to choose a payment provider, marketplace payments compared with standard card payments
  • Proof pages: one case study and one product page that the cluster links to

Start with the clusters closest to what you sell. Build one well before you start the next.

What content formats work in B2B SEO?

Pick the format that fits the question.

  • Guides. For broad questions that need depth, such as "how to choose" or "how to set up."
  • Checklists and templates. Easy to use and to link to. Good for lead capture if the value is real.
  • Comparison pages. For buyers weighing two options. State the facts and be fair about where a rival is better.
  • Case studies. A client's problem, what you did and the result in real numbers, with permission to share.
  • Glossary pages. Short, clear answers to "what is" searches.
  • Original research. Data from your own work that others cite and link to.
  • Video and webinars. Useful for demos and expert talks. Add a written summary so the content can be found.

How long does B2B SEO take?

Google says some changes show in hours and others take months, and it suggests waiting a few weeks before you judge a change. B2B adds a second delay: the sales cycle. A page may rank within months and the deal it influences may close months after that.

A reasonable expectation is:

  • Months 1 to 3: audit, fixes, keyword plan and first pages
  • Months 4 to 6: early rankings for smaller searches and the first enquiries
  • Months 7 to 12: growth in rankings, more pages and clearer data on leads

These ranges are a planning guide, not a promise. Your market, site and effort change the pace.

How much does B2B SEO cost?

There is no standard price. It depends on the size of your site, how competitive your market is, how much content you need and how much your team does. The cost has three parts: people (your team, a freelancer or an agency), tools (keyword, audit and reporting software) and content and links (writing, design and outreach).

When you compare quotes, compare the scope. A low price may not include writing, links or reporting. Ask what you get each month and how it ties to enquiries.

How do you choose between focusing on SaaS, services or manufacturing?

The approach changes slightly by sector.

  • B2B software (SaaS): focus on use cases, integrations, comparisons and "alternative to" searches. Free trials and demos are the main conversions.
  • Professional services: focus on expertise. Named experts, case studies and guides by topic build trust. Conversions are enquiries and calls.
  • Manufacturing and industrial: focus on product specifications, applications, standards and supplier searches. Clear data sheets and catalog pages matter.
  • Healthcare and life sciences: focus on accuracy, sources and compliance. Review claims carefully before you publish.

How should B2B SEO handle AI search?

Buyers now ask AI tools such as ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI answers for vendor ideas and explanations. These tools pull from pages that are clear, factual and well sourced. To fit:

  • Put a short, direct answer at the top of each page.
  • Use clear headings, lists and tables.
  • Keep facts consistent across your site, profiles and listings.
  • Be specific about who you serve and what you do. Vague copy gives AI nothing to repeat.
  • Earn independent mentions in reviews, press and partner pages.
  • Check what AI tools say about you. Ask them your buyers' questions and note where you are missing or wrong.

No method guarantees a mention. The steps above also help normal search, so they are worth doing either way.

How do you measure B2B SEO?

Measure what the business cares about. A simple set:

  • Organic traffic to pages that matter, not all pages
  • Rankings for a short list of key searches
  • Conversions: demo requests, contact forms, downloads and calls from organic search
  • Lead quality: the share that sales accepts
  • Pipeline and revenue influenced by search, tracked in your CRM
  • Search Console data: impressions, clicks and the searches that bring them

Because B2B deals are slow, a lead from search may close months later. Connect your analytics to your CRM so you can trace a deal back to its first search visit.

How do you run a B2B SEO audit?

A B2B SEO audit is a structured check of your site and search presence. You can do a first pass yourself in a day. Use this order:

  1. Check indexing. In Search Console, see how many pages are indexed and why others are not. Compare with the pages you want to rank.
  2. List your top pages. Which pages bring visits and which bring enquiries? Often they are different pages.
  3. Find gaps. Search your 10 most important buyer questions in Google. Note who ranks and what type of page it is.
  4. Review page quality. For each key page, ask if it answers the question fully, names the author, shows proof and has a clear next step.
  5. Check links. See who links to you and to your three closest rivals. Look for sources that link to them but not to you.
  6. Test speed and mobile. Open your key pages on a phone and note slow or broken parts.
  7. Write the fix list. Sort it into quick fixes, pages to build and long-term work.

Keep the audit as a living file. Update it each quarter so you can see progress.

How do you work with sales on B2B SEO?

Sales teams hear the exact words buyers use. Use them.

  • Ask sales for the top 20 questions buyers ask in first calls and write a page for each.
  • Collect objections. "How do you compare with X?" and "Is it secure?" are page ideas.
  • Share new pages with sales so they can send them to buyers. A page that helps in a call often ranks well too.
  • Ask sales to tag the source of every lead, so you can see which search pages helped.
  • Meet monthly for 30 minutes to review leads from search and decide what to write next.

In-house or agency?

Small teams often start in-house with one marketer and a freelance writer. Larger programs may need an agency for strategy, writing, technical work and reporting. Many companies mix both: the agency sets the plan and the in-house team supplies product knowledge. Ask for a written scope, who writes the content, how results tie to leads and how you can end the contract.

Common B2B SEO mistakes

  • Chasing traffic. Visits from the wrong audience do not become enquiries.
  • Writing only top-of-funnel posts. Buyers near a decision need comparison, pricing and proof pages.
  • Skipping the buying group. Content for users only misses the people who approve.
  • Thin or generic pages. "We offer solutions" tells nobody anything.
  • No proof. Claims without case studies, names or numbers do not convince.
  • Ignoring technical health. Pages that are not indexed cannot rank.
  • Expecting fast results. Plan for months.
  • Never updating. Old pages lose ground. Refresh key pages at least once a year.

A quick checklist

  • Buyers and their questions written down
  • Goals tied to leads and pipeline
  • An audit of technical health, content, keywords and links
  • Keywords mapped to each buyer stage
  • Pages for products, industries, use cases, comparisons, pricing and proof
  • Named authors, sources and a last-updated date on each page
  • Honest link building
  • Reports that tie search to enquiries and deals

Final thoughts

B2B SEO works when it follows the way your buyers actually buy. Know who is involved, answer their questions at each stage and prove your claims. Then measure leads, not only visits, and improve the pages that move deals forward.
