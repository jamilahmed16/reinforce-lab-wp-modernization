Answer engine optimization, or AEO, is the work of getting your brand and your pages used as the answer when someone asks a question. SEO aims to rank your pages in a list of results. AEO aims to be the source that a search tool, a voice assistant or an AI chatbot quotes, names or links when it gives a direct answer. The two share one foundation. AEO adds a focus on short, clear answers, on facts that other sources agree with, and on being mentioned across the web.

This guide explains AEO against SEO in plain words. You will see what each one is, what stays the same, what AEO adds, how AEO differs from GEO, and what to do about schema, featured snippets and voice. It has two worked examples, a step-by-step plan, a measurement method and a 90-day timeline. Google's own view comes first, because it shapes everything else: for Google Search, optimizing for AI answers is still SEO.

What is answer engine optimization (AEO)?

Answer engine optimization is the practice of making your content visible and useful to systems that give direct answers. Those systems include Google's featured snippets and AI Overviews, voice assistants, and chat tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Claude. Ahrefs describes AEO as being mentioned and cited directly in answers, rather than only ranking pages. It also calls AEO a specialized subset of SEO, not a replacement.

An "answer engine" is any tool that responds to a question with an answer instead of a list of links. The idea is not new. Featured snippets, the "People also ask" box and voice assistants have given direct answers for years. What changed is the scale. AI tools now write full answers, and they often draw on several pages at once.

Two terms you will see next to AEO are GEO and LLM optimization. We use them like this:

  • AEO is the broad idea of being the answer, including snippets, voice and AI tools.
  • GEO (generative engine optimization) focuses on being cited in answers that AI tools write. Read our guide to what generative engine optimization is.
  • LLM optimization focuses on how large language models use and describe your brand.

In practice the work overlaps almost completely. Pick one name for your plan and move on to the work.

What is the difference between AEO and SEO?

SEO is about ranking pages and earning clicks. AEO is about being the answer, whether or not anyone clicks. SEO treats the page as the unit. AEO treats a passage, a fact or your brand as the unit.

What stays the same?

Google says that to appear as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode, a page must be indexed and eligible to show in Google Search with a snippet. It adds that there are no extra technical requirements. The same SEO basics apply: allow crawling, link pages together, give people a good page experience, keep important content in text and keep structured data matched to the visible page.

  • Access. A blocked or unindexed page cannot rank and cannot be retrieved for an answer.
  • Useful content. A page that answers a real need well helps in both.
  • Search intent. Whether someone types a keyword or asks a question, you still need to match what they want.
  • Trust. Real authors, honest facts and a reputation beyond your site help in both.
  • A working site. Slow or broken pages hurt both.

So the answer to "do I need AEO if I already do SEO?" is that good SEO gets you most of the way. AEO is the layer that checks whether your answers are clear and quotable, and whether other sources agree with you.

What does AEO add on top of SEO?

Five habits matter most.

1. Answer first

Put a direct answer in the first one or two sentences under each question heading. Write it so it makes sense out of context. A snippet, an AI tool or a voice assistant can then use it without the rest of the page. Ahrefs reports that 44.2% of the citations it studied came from the first 30% of a page. That figure comes from Ahrefs' own data, so treat it as a pointer, not a law. The advice is simple and costs nothing: do not bury your answer.

2. Questions as headings

Headings that match how people ask make a page easier to match. Take the questions from sales calls, support emails and the "People also ask" box. Use the wording your buyers use.

3. Specific, sourced facts

Numbers, dates and named sources give a tool something it can check. A claim with a source and a year is easier to trust than a general one.

4. Clear entities

An entity is a clearly identified thing, such as your company, a product or a person. Use one brand name, and keep your services, location and leadership facts the same on your site, your profiles and your listings. If the facts conflict, a tool may leave you out or describe you wrongly.

5. Mentions beyond your site

Answer tools do not only read your pages. They also weigh what the wider web says about you. Reviews, lists, news pages and directories that mention you give a tool a second source. Ahrefs says that most brand mentions in AI answers come from third-party sites. Earn these through real stories and real data. Google says seeking fake mentions is not helpful.

Do you still need to rank to be the answer?

Ranking helps, but it is no longer the only way in. Ahrefs found that about 76% of pages cited in Google AI Overviews also ranked in the top 10 in July 2025, and about 38% in March 2026. It also found that, on average, about 12% of citations from AI assistants ranked in Google's top 10 for the original prompt. These figures come from Ahrefs' own studies and cover different tools and dates, so use them as a direction and not as a target.

The cause is probably query fan-out. A tool splits one question into several related searches, and a page that answers one of those sub-questions can be cited even if it does not rank for the main question. Google confirms that AI Overviews and AI Mode may use query fan-out. We cover the numbers in more detail in our GEO vs SEO guide.

The lesson for AEO is to cover a topic in full. A set of pages that answer the related questions gives these searches more places to find you.

What about schema, FAQ markup and featured snippets?

FAQ markup

Many older AEO guides tell you to add FAQ schema to win snippets. Google changed this. Its documentation updates list the FAQ rich result as deprecated: it stopped appearing in Google Search from 7 May 2026. You can still write FAQ sections for your readers, and a clear question and answer pair is still easy for a tool to quote. But do not expect a special FAQ result from Google.

Structured data in general

Google says structured data is not required for its AI features and that no special markup is needed. It still recommends using it as part of SEO, because it helps with eligibility for rich results. Keep the markup honest and matched to the text on the page.

Featured snippets

A featured snippet is a box that shows the answer text first, above the usual results. Google decides which pages appear there, and clicking one takes the reader to the matching section of the page. You cannot buy the spot. You can write a clear, direct answer under a heading that matches the question, and then see whether Google uses it.

Voice answers

Voice assistants read out short answers. The same habits help: a short, plain answer near the top, a clear question heading and accurate business details. If you are a local business, keep your Google Business Profile and listings correct.

llms.txt and "chunking"

Google says it does not use llms.txt files and that there is no need to break content into tiny chunks for AI. Ahrefs likewise tells readers to skip llms.txt. If you want the file for other services, it is harmless, but it is not a priority.

Worked example 1: an industrial supplier

This is an illustration, not a client result. Imagine a company that sells stainless steel fasteners to manufacturers. Its product pages rank well. Buyers also ask detailed questions, such as "which stainless grade resists salt spray best" and "what is the difference between A2 and A4 bolts".

The team tests 18 of these questions in four AI tools and in Google. For most of them, the answers name competitors, a standards body and two trade sites. The supplier is named in 2 of 72 answers.

The gap is clear. The product pages list sizes and prices but do not answer the technical questions. The team plans one page for each question that buyers raise. Each page opens with a two-sentence answer, adds a short table of grades with the standard named as the source, and ends with a note on when to ask an engineer. The head of engineering signs each page as author.

They keep the product pages as they are. They also check that the company name, address and product range read the same on the website, the trade directory and the profile pages. At the monthly test they compare against the baseline. They watch for more named mentions, for cited pages that are theirs and for visits from AI tool domains in analytics.

Worked example 2: a software help center

This is another illustration. Imagine a small company that makes scheduling software. Its help center has hundreds of articles. Customers and prospects ask AI tools "how do I set up recurring shifts in this tool" and "does this tool work with payroll software".

In tests, the AI tools give a mix of answers, some of them out of date. One tool describes a feature that was removed a year ago. The team traces it to an old article that still ranks and to a third-party review that was never updated.

The fixes are specific. The team updates the old article and adds a clear date. It rewrites the setup article so the first lines state the steps in order. It adds a short "what changed" note where features were removed. It also asks the review site to correct its page and sends the reviewer the updated feature list.

The lesson is that AEO includes accuracy. If tools describe your product wrongly, the fix is often to update your own pages and the third-party pages they rely on.

How do you do AEO step by step?

  1. Check access. Make sure Googlebot and the AI crawlers you want can reach your pages. Check robots.txt, your firewall and your CDN, which can block bots by default.
  2. Check indexing. In Search Console, confirm your key pages are indexed and eligible to show with a snippet.
  3. List the questions. Write 15 to 25 questions your buyers ask. Include "best X for Y" questions, brand questions and technical questions.
  4. Test them. Ask each question in Google, ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity. Record whether you are named, whether you are linked, which pages are cited and who else appears.
  5. Fix wrong facts. Where an answer is wrong, trace the source. Update your own page, or the third-party page, or both.
  6. Rewrite the key pages. Add a direct answer at the top, question headings, a named source or two and an honest update date.
  7. Fill the gaps. Where answers cite others and you have no page, write one useful page. Where a competitor is named in a list and you are not, plan one real story to pitch.
  8. Clean up your entity facts. Make your name, services, location and leaders read the same everywhere.
  9. Measure and repeat. Run the same tests every month and record the trend.

How do you measure AEO?

Use one report with two parts, SEO and AEO.

  • Prompt tests. Run the same 15 to 25 questions monthly in the same tools. Record mentions, links, cited pages and competitors. AI answers change from run to run, so read the trend.
  • Mention share and citation share. The share of answers that name your brand, and the share that cite your domain.
  • Brand accuracy. The share of answers about you that get the facts right.
  • AI referral visits. In analytics, filter visits from AI tool domains such as chatgpt.com or perplexity.ai. Check your own data to see which domains appear.
  • Search Console. Google reports AI feature traffic there, together with the rest of your search data.
  • Business results. Add a "how did you hear about us" field to forms.

Be careful with paid tools. They can help you run prompts at scale, but none can see inside Google's systems, and Google warns about tools that claim to. There is also no public prompt volume for AI tools, so you cannot size a topic the way you can with keyword tools. Use your own sales and support questions.

What are the common AEO mistakes?

  • Trusting old advice. Some guides, including recent ones, still promise FAQ-based wins. Check the date and check Google's documentation.
  • Using unsourced statistics. Many AEO pages quote exact multipliers with no source. Quote only what you can trace to a study.
  • Rewriting everything for tools. Google says you do not need to write in a special way for AI. Write for your readers.
  • Making thin pages for every variation. Google warns that doing this mainly to influence AI answers can break its spam policy.
  • Ignoring SEO. If your pages are not indexed, AEO has nothing to work with.
  • Not checking accuracy. A wrong answer about your product can cost more than a missed mention.
  • Testing once. Answers vary. One test proves little.

A 90-day AEO plan

Days 1 to 30: access and baseline

Fix crawling and indexing problems. Write your question list. Run the first tests in four tools. Record the baseline and list every wrong fact you find.

Days 31 to 60: fix and rewrite

Correct wrong facts on your own pages and on key third-party pages. Rewrite your five most important pages with direct answers, question headings, sources and update dates. Write one or two pages for questions where you are missing.

Days 61 to 90: earn and review

Pitch one or two real stories to sites that AI tools already cite for your topic. Rerun the tests. Compare against the baseline. Decide which fixes to repeat. Keep a steady pace of a few pages a month, because quality and updates matter more than volume.
