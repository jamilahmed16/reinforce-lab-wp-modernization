LLM SEO is the work of making your brand and your pages easy for large language models to find, understand and cite when they answer questions. It is also called LLM optimization or LLMO. It is not a new trick. For Google, it is ordinary SEO plus a few extra jobs: clear facts, quotable pages, a good presence on other sites, and a way to check what the models say about you.

This guide explains what LLM SEO is, how LLMs choose what to cite, and what the evidence shows. It also covers what Google says you can ignore, and gives a nine-step plan, a worked example and an honest answer on how long it takes. We cite only studies and documents we could read at the source, and we say when a finding is a correlation, a vendor view or a single test.

What is LLM SEO?

LLM SEO means improving how often and how accurately large language models mention your brand, recommend your products and cite your pages. The goal is not a rank. The goal is to be part of the answer when someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity or Google's AI features about your category.

A language model can know about you in two ways. The first is training: the model learned from text in its training data, collected before it was released, so what it says reflects what was on the web then. The second is retrieval: when the model needs fresh facts, it searches the web, reads some pages and writes an answer from them. Google calls the second method retrieval-augmented generation, or grounding, and describes it as relying on its core Search ranking systems to find relevant, up-to-date pages. Most of the work you can control today is about retrieval, because you can change a page this week but not a model that was trained last year.

These names overlap, and different writers use them in different ways. The table shows the common use.

How is LLM SEO different from traditional SEO?

Traditional SEO aims to rank pages in search results and win clicks. LLM SEO aims to get your brand named and your pages cited inside an answer. They share a base, and a site that is weak at traditional SEO is rarely strong at LLM SEO.

AreaTraditional SEOLLM SEO
GoalRankings and clicksMentions and citations in answers
Query styleShort keywordsLonger, conversational prompts and follow-ups
Authority signalsBacklinks, content qualityBacklinks and quality, plus brand mentions and consistent facts across other sites
ContentPages that match intentPages with clear headings, answer-first passages and sourced evidence
MeasurementRank, traffic, conversionsMention rate, citation rate and share of voice across a fixed prompt set

How do LLMs choose what to cite?

Nobody outside the AI companies knows the exact rules, and they do not publish them. What the companies do say is limited. Google says its AI features use a technique called query fan-out: the model splits one question into several related searches and gathers pages for each. Its example is a question about fixing a lawn full of weeds, which fans out into searches on herbicides, chemical-free removal and prevention. So a page can be retrieved for a question it never named.

From the documents we read, three things seem to matter. These are our reading and not a published formula:

  • Retrievability. The page must be crawlable, indexed and readable in the HTML. For Google, a page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet. Other tools use their own crawlers or search indexes.
  • Relevance and clarity. The page must answer the question, or one of the fan-out questions, in a passage that makes sense alone.
  • Trust. The page and the brand should look reliable, with named sources, named authors, current facts and agreement from other sites.

What does the evidence say?

There is less hard evidence than the volume of advice suggests. These are the main studies and documents we found, with their limits.

  • Adding evidence can raise visibility, in a test. Researchers from Princeton, IIT Delhi and others built a benchmark called GEO-bench of 10,000 queries and tested nine ways to change content. They reported that adding citations, quotations from relevant sources and statistics boosted source visibility by up to 40% in their tests, and that the methods worked on Perplexity with gains of up to 37%. The effect varied by domain. It is a controlled study from 2024, published at the KDD 2024 conference, not a guarantee for your site.
  • Fresher pages are cited more. Ahrefs analyzed 17 million citations across seven AI search platforms. It found that content cited in AI answers was 25.7% fresher than content in regular results, and that assistants showed a 13.1% preference for more recently updated content. That is a correlation from one vendor's data set.
  • Answers vary. SparkToro had 600 volunteers run 12 prompts through ChatGPT, Claude and Google's AI features 2,961 times. There was less than a 1 in 100 chance of the same list of brands twice and about a 1 in 1,000 chance of the same order. So measure a rate across many prompts, not a rank.
  • Most AI crawlers do not run JavaScript. In December 2024, Vercel reported that none of the major AI crawlers it tested rendered JavaScript, including those from OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity. Content that loads only in the browser may be invisible to them. That is a snapshot from one network, so test your own pages.

What does Google say you can ignore?

Google's guide to generative AI features has a section on myths. For Google Search, it says you do not need:

  • llms.txt files or other special files and markup. Google says Search does not use them. It adds that making one is fine for other systems that use it, and will neither help nor harm your visibility in Google Search. See our llms.txt guide.
  • "Chunking" content into tiny pieces. Google says there is no requirement to do this and no ideal page length.
  • Rewriting content only for AI. Google says its systems understand synonyms and meaning, so you do not need every long-tail variation.
  • Inauthentic mentions. Google says seeking them is not as helpful as it might seem.
  • Special structured data. It is not required, though still useful for rich results.

This is Google's view of Google Search. Other tools may behave differently, and Google also says that no third-party tool has access to its internal ranking or AI systems. It is a good check against advice that sounds technical but has no source.

A nine-step LLM SEO plan

Step 1: Make sure models can reach and read your pages

Check that key pages are indexed, not blocked by robots.txt or your firewall, and show their main text in the raw HTML. OpenAI says sites that opt out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers. Anthropic says disabling Claude-SearchBot or Claude-User may reduce your visibility in Claude answers. Our robots.txt guide explains the file, and an AI search audit tests each bot.

Step 2: Write a list of buyer prompts

Write 10 to 15 questions in the words your buyers use. Include category questions, comparison questions and a few about your brand. This list is your keyword research for LLMs. It is different from classic keyword research because prompts are longer and more like speech. Semrush offers a Prompt Research feature in its AI toolkit, and you can also use sales calls, support tickets and Search Console queries.

Step 3: Put the answer first

Start each section with one or two sentences that answer the heading's question. Then add detail. A passage that makes sense on its own is easier to quote. Do not break pages into tiny fragments for the sake of it, since Google says that is not needed. Just make each section clear.

Step 4: Add evidence

Back claims with a named statistic, a quote from an expert or a link to a source, each with a date. This is the one tactic that has controlled test evidence behind it. Use only figures you have read at the source and say what the study can and cannot show.

Step 5: Fix your facts and your entity

Keep one set of facts: your name, what you do, who you serve, where you work, prices or price ranges, and who runs the company. Make your site, LinkedIn page and directory listings match. A model that sees three different descriptions of you will repeat one of them, and not always the right one.

Step 6: Keep important pages current

Update pages whose facts change, such as pricing, features, rules and benchmarks. Show a real last-updated date and change something real when you update. Ahrefs' data suggests fresher pages are cited more, though that is a correlation.

Step 7: Build your presence on other sites

Answers often rely on third-party sources: review sites, directories, news, community threads and comparison pages. Run your prompts, list the sites the answers cite, and ask which of them you could be on in an honest way. Do not buy fake reviews or post disguised ads. Google says inauthentic mentions are not as useful as they might seem.

Step 8: Measure what you can

Run your prompt list each month, three runs per prompt, and record mentions, citations, descriptions and competitors. Use Google's Generative AI performance report in Search Console, which reports impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode. Use Bing Webmaster Tools' AI Performance report for citations in Copilot. Our guide to checking if AI tools mention your brand and our list of AI brand visibility tools help with this step.

Step 9: Repeat and keep the record

Repeat the test each quarter and after big site changes. Keep every sheet. The point is to see a trend and to learn which changes helped.

Worked example: a service firm builds an LLM SEO plan

This is an illustration, not a client result. Imagine an eight-person firm that offers payroll services to restaurants. It writes 12 prompts, such as "payroll service for a small restaurant" and "payroll provider vs doing payroll in house". It runs each three times in three tools, which gives 108 answers, and is named in 9, about 8%.

The source map shows the answers citing two directory sites, a trade association page and a competitor's guide. The firm is on none of them. Its own services page says "we help businesses grow" and gives no price or location.

The plan has four jobs. It rewrites the services page so the first lines say who the firm serves, what it does and a price range. It adds one page with a clear answer to the in-house question, with two sourced statistics. It completes its directory profiles and asks three customers for honest reviews. It tests the same 108 runs after 60 days. The firm records the result whatever it is, because the point is a fair comparison, not a promise.

LLM SEO for service businesses

Service firms have an advantage and a risk. The advantage is that buyers ask specific questions, such as "who does X in my city" or "how much does Y cost". A page with a clear answer can win those. The risk is thin pages. A services page with vague claims gives a model little to quote. Include who you serve, where, what you do, a price range or the factors that set the price, and proof such as named reviews or credentials. Keep your directory and map listings accurate, because answers about local and service firms often rely on them.

Which LLM SEO tools help?

You can start with no paid tool: a spreadsheet, the AI tools, Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools. SEO suites such as Ahrefs and Semrush offer AI visibility features, and stand-alone trackers exist. Test any tool against your hand results and treat a precise rank inside AI answers with caution. Do you need an LLM SEO agency?

Not to start. The plan above can be run in-house. An agency helps when you lack the time, when the fixes need a developer, or when the source map shows work such as outreach that you cannot do alone. Ask what you will receive: the prompt list, raw answers, the source map, a ranked list of fixes and a date to repeat the test. Be wary of any agency that promises a place in AI answers, because no one can guarantee it. Our LLM optimization service and SEO and AI search audit follow this approach.

How long does LLM SEO take to work?

No study we found gives a reliable time. Fixes to access and facts can show in retrieval-based answers within weeks, since OpenAI says a robots.txt change takes about 24 hours to apply for search. Gains from new content and outside mentions take longer, and training-based knowledge changes only when models are retrained. Treat any firm promise of a timeline as a red flag, and judge progress by your own repeated test.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Chasing a rank. The order of brands in answers changes from run to run.
  • Adding files and markup first. Fix access, facts and content before you add anything special.
  • Writing thin pages for every prompt. Google warns that making pages for each variation to manipulate AI answers breaks its scaled content abuse policy.
  • Using unsourced statistics. An unsourced number is a risk to readers and to models.
  • Ignoring other sites. Answers often come from third-party pages.
  • Testing once. One answer is a clue, not a result.

Final thoughts

LLM SEO is mostly good SEO with clearer facts, better evidence and a plan to check results. Start with access, then facts, then pages that answer questions with sources. Build a real presence on the sites the answers cite, and measure with a fixed prompt list. Ignore advice that has no source, including extra files, until the basics work.
