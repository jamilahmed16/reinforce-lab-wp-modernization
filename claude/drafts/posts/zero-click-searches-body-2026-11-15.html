A zero click search is a search that ends without the person clicking any result. Some are answered on the results page, such as a weather check, a store opening time, a definition or an AI summary. Others end because the person changes the query or gives up. You cannot avoid zero click searches, and no one can stop Google from answering questions on its own page. What you can do is be the source those answers use, be named in them, and earn the clicks that still happen.

This guide explains what zero click searches are, how common they are, what the data says about clicks and AI summaries, and how to win anyway. It covers the content that tends to be used in answers, how to measure value without clicks, when zero click visibility hurts and a worked example. We cite only studies we could read at the publisher, and we state the limits of each. This page replaces an older post on the same address.

What is a zero click search?

A zero click search is a search on which the person never clicks a result. SparkToro, whose 2024 study is the best-known count, defines it as a search that ends without clicking on any of the results presented. That includes searches where the person was satisfied, searches where they were frustrated, and searches where they changed the query and tried again. So "zero click" does not always mean the page gave a full answer.

The answer can come from several places on the page: a featured snippet, a knowledge panel, a weather or sports box, a map pack, "People also ask", a calculator or, increasingly, an AI summary such as Google's AI Overviews. People also get answers from AI tools such as ChatGPT, where there is no results page at all.

How common are zero click searches?

Three studies give a picture. They use different methods, so do not add them together.

  • SparkToro and Datos (2024). Using clickstream data from Datos, a Semrush company, for September 2022 to May 2024, SparkToro found that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended with zero clicks. About 37% of searches ended the session, and about 22% led to a new query. SparkToro's own headline is that, of every 1,000 US Google searches, 374 clicks go to the open web. Limits: the panel has little coverage of iPhones and iPads, mobile data starts in January 2024, ad blockers may distort paid search counts, and SparkToro says it is likely undercounting zero click behavior because it misses voice answers and the Google app.
  • Pew Research Center (2025). Pew tracked the browsing of 900 US adults in March 2025, covering 68,879 Google searches, of which 12,593 produced an AI summary. People clicked a regular result link in 8% of visits to pages with an AI summary and 15% of visits to pages without one, and clicked a link inside the summary in 1% of visits. Around two-thirds of all searches ended in either more browsing on Google or leaving without a click. Google disputes Pew's method and says it does not see significant drops in aggregate web traffic.
  • Bain and Dynata (2024). Bain surveyed 1,117 consumers in December 2024 and reported that about 80% rely on zero click results in at least 40% of their searches, and estimated that this reduces organic web traffic by 15% to 25%. It also said about 60% of searches on traditional engines now end without a visit to another site. This is a survey with an estimate attached, and the traffic figure is Bain's own estimate.

The direction is clear in all three: many searches end on the results page, and AI summaries make that more likely. The exact size depends on the method, the country and the type of search. Informational searches are affected most.

Do zero click searches hurt SEO?

They change what SEO is worth, but they do not end it. If your business depends on clicks for simple questions, such as "what is X" or "opening hours", you will see less traffic from those queries. If your business depends on buyers who compare, decide and contact you, those searches still produce clicks, and the answers you appear in can add awareness before the click. Bain's view is that SEO alone will not be enough, and that brands should keep refining it while testing how to be visible in AI summaries and AI tools.

So the useful questions are these. Which of your pages target queries that Google answers on its own page? Does a click on those pages make you money? And could being named in the answer help you in ways you can measure?

How do you win anyway?

1. Be the source of the answer

For queries that show a featured snippet or an AI summary, write the answer in the first sentence or two under a clear heading, then add detail. Use the question as the heading when it reads naturally, keep paragraphs short and add lists and tables where they help. Our guide to how to rank in AI Overviews explains what Google says about eligibility: a page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet.

2. Give people a reason to click

An answer box shows a fact. A click is worth it when the page offers more: a worked example, a template, a calculator, original data, a comparison or a way to take the next step. Write the first lines so that they answer, and the rest so that it earns the visit. Do not hide the answer to force a click, because it harms trust and may lose the snippet.

3. Target searches where clicks still happen

Buying and comparison searches, such as "best X for Y", "X vs Y" and "X pricing", tend to produce more clicks than simple fact questions. Pew found that clicks to regular results fall when an AI summary appears. We expect buying and comparison searches to hold up better than simple facts, but we found no study that measures this, so check your own Search Console data. Build your keyword plan around searches where a visit creates value, and treat simple fact queries as awareness.

4. Be named, not only linked

People who see your brand in an answer may search for you later, visit directly or ask a colleague. Build the signals that lead to a mention: consistent facts about your business across your site and profiles, honest reviews, and mentions on the sites that answers cite. Our guides to checking if AI tools mention your brand and generative engine optimization cover this work.

5. Use your own listings

For local businesses, many zero click answers are map packs and business profiles. Keep your Google Business Profile complete and accurate, answer questions on it, add photos and respond to reviews. Calls and direction requests count as results even though no one visits your website.

6. Own the channels you control

Give searchers a way to stay in touch: an email list, a useful tool or a community. If a click is rarer, each visit is worth more to you when you capture the relationship.

Which searches are hit hardest?

Informational searches that have a short, stable answer are hit hardest. Examples are definitions, facts, conversions, times and simple how-to steps. Searches that need a choice, a comparison, a price or a person, such as "best accountant for a small restaurant", still send people to pages, because an AI summary cannot make the decision for them. Studies agree on the direction, but we found no study that splits the effect by industry, so use your own Search Console data. Filter by query type, such as questions versus "best" and "vs" terms, and see which groups lost clicks while holding impressions. Those are the pages where an answer box or summary is taking your visit.

For these pages, decide between three options. You can keep the page and strengthen it with something worth a visit. You can merge it into a stronger guide. Or you can leave it as is, because the page still wins awareness and costs nothing to keep. Do not delete pages in a hurry. Changes to live URLs on a business site need a plan for redirects and a check of what the page earns.

How do you measure value without clicks?

Clicks and sessions no longer tell the whole story. Track these as well:

  • Impressions and click-through rate in Search Console, by page and query. A page can gain impressions while clicks fall, which may mean you appear in answers.
  • Google's Generative AI performance report in Search Console, which shows impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode.
  • Branded search and direct traffic, which may rise when people see your name in answers.
  • Mentions and citations across a fixed prompt list in AI tools. SparkToro found less than a 1 in 100 chance that the same prompt returns the same list of brands twice, so run each prompt several times.
  • Business results: leads, calls, direction requests and sales, tracked by source, with a "how did you hear about us" question.

Be careful with causes. If clicks fall and impressions rise, an AI summary may be the reason, but seasons, rankings and competitors also change results. Check the queries before you decide.

When does zero click visibility hurt?

Zero click visibility is a poor goal when your content only gives away something you sell by the click, such as a simple fact that your site monetizes with ads, or when you lack any way to benefit from awareness. It is a good goal when buyers who see your name later contact you, when you sell something that the answer cannot give, or when your brand is trying to be known in a market. Decide by page. For each important page, ask what a visit is worth, what an appearance in an answer is worth, and which one you can measure.

A short checklist helps for each important page. What question does the page answer? Does Google answer it on the results page? What does a visit earn you today? What would an appearance in the answer earn you? If a visit earns little and an appearance earns nothing you can measure, the page is a candidate for a merge. If a visit earns a lead, protect it and add the reasons to click that the answer box cannot give.

Is zero click bad for users?

Often it is good. A quick answer saves time when the question is simple, such as a time zone or a unit conversion. It can be bad when the answer is wrong, thin or lacks context, or when it hides the original source. Google's AI features show links, and OpenAI says ChatGPT search results and citations can be incomplete, outdated or incorrect. Readers should open a source when accuracy matters, and publishers should make their sources clear and their facts current.

Worked example: a software review site

This is an illustration, not a client result. Imagine a site that explains accounting software to small businesses. Its definition pages, such as "what is accrual accounting", lost clicks over the past year, while impressions stayed flat. Its comparison pages still bring leads.

The team lists 40 pages and sorts them by what a visit is worth. For 12 definition pages, it keeps a clear answer at the top, adds a worked example and a template, and links to the related comparison. It stops writing new pages for simple facts. For 10 comparison pages, it updates tables, dates and sources. It claims its profiles on two software review sites, since AI answers cite them. It tests 15 buyer questions three times each in three AI tools, and records whether the site is named and cited.

After 90 days it compares impressions, clicks, newsletter signups and demo requests for each group with the starting numbers, and the prompt results with the baseline. If definition pages still lose clicks but bring signups through the template, it keeps them. If not, it merges them into stronger guides.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Trying to avoid zero click searches. You cannot control what Google shows. Aim to be in the answer and earn the next step.
  • Hiding the answer to force a click. It reduces trust and the chance of being quoted.
  • Judging success by clicks alone. Track impressions, mentions, branded search and leads.
  • Making many thin pages for simple facts. Google calls pages made to manipulate results scaled content abuse.
  • Ignoring your own profiles. For local and service searches, listings often are the answer.
  • Treating studies as exact. Each has limits, and numbers differ by method.

Final thoughts

Zero click searches are part of search now, and AI summaries add to them. Do not fight them. Answer first so you can be the source, give people a reason to visit, aim at searches where a visit pays, build your name across the web and measure results beyond clicks. Decide page by page what an appearance and a visit are worth.
