GEO for law firms means making your firm and your lawyers easy for AI tools to find, understand and name accurately when people ask legal questions. GEO stands for generative engine optimization. In practice it is good SEO plus clear firm facts, answer-first practice pages, strong lawyer bios, honest profiles on the sites AI answers cite, and a monthly check of what the tools say. It must also follow your advertising rules, because a false or misleading claim is a risk whether a person or an AI tool repeats it.

This guide explains how GEO works for a firm, what the data shows, and a plan you can run. It includes a worked example and a short section on choosing an agency. This is general marketing information, not legal advice. Advertising rules differ by state and country, so check your own bar or regulator before you publish. We cite only sources we could read at the publisher, and we say when a figure is a survey or a test.

What is GEO for law firms?

Generative engine optimization is work that helps AI tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews cite and recommend your content. For a law firm it covers two kinds of questions. The first is informational: "how long do I have to file a claim", "do I need a lawyer for a slip and fall", "what does probate cost". The second is a shopping question: "best family lawyer near me" or "what should I ask a divorce lawyer".

Our guide to generative engine optimization explains the general method. This article applies it to legal services, where trust, accuracy and ethics rules matter more than in most fields.

Why does it matter for law firms?

Clients are already using AI. Clio's 2025 Legal Trends Report surveyed 1,000 US adults, a sample that matches the US population by age, gender, region, income and race. It found that 24% had already used AI to answer a legal question, and another 40% would consider it. 46% said they would consult AI before contacting a lawyer. Of those who had used AI for a legal question, 28% were directed by the AI to contact a lawyer, and in 12% of cases the AI convinced them their problem was not worth pursuing. Clio also found that more consumers say they would look for their next lawyer online. This is a survey of self-reported behavior by a software company, so read it as a direction, not an exact count.

The same report notes that many consumers asked AI how to find "the best" lawyer. If AI tools are shaping how people shop, your firm needs to be named correctly in those answers.

What do the advertising rules say?

In the US, ABA Model Rule 7.1 says a lawyer shall not make a false or misleading communication about the lawyer or the lawyer's services. It adds that a communication is false or misleading if it contains a material misrepresentation of fact or law, or leaves out a fact needed to keep the statement from being misleading as a whole. States adopt their own versions, and many add rules on testimonials, specialist claims and past results. Check yours.

For GEO, the practical meaning is simple. Everything on your site and your profiles can be quoted by an AI tool, so every claim must be accurate, current and supportable. That includes:

  • No promise or implied guarantee of a result.
  • No "best" or "top" claim you cannot support.
  • Past results stated with the context your bar requires.
  • No specialist or certified label that your jurisdiction does not allow.
  • A lawyer reviews every page, including pages drafted with AI help.

Never write GEO content that is designed to mislead a model. Even if it worked, it would put your license and your reputation at risk.

What does the evidence say about getting cited?

  • Evidence in the page helps, in a test. A Princeton-led team tested nine content changes on a 10,000-query benchmark. They reported that adding citations, quotations from relevant sources and statistics boosted visibility by up to 40%, with gains up to 37% on Perplexity. The effect varied by domain, and the study dates from 2024. For lawyers, the natural version is to cite the statute, the court rule or the agency page.
  • Answers change from run to run. SparkToro had 600 volunteers run 12 prompts through ChatGPT, Claude and Google's AI features 2,961 times. There was less than a 1 in 100 chance of the same list of brands twice. So measure how often you appear across many prompts, and do not chase a rank.
  • Crawl access can decide it. OpenAI says sites that opt out of OAI-SearchBot are not shown in ChatGPT search answers. Security plugins and CDN settings sometimes block bots without anyone noticing.
  • Google adds no special requirements. To be a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode, a page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet, and Google lists no additional technical requirements.

An eight-step GEO plan for a law firm

Step 1: Let the right crawlers in

Open your robots.txt and your security settings. Make sure Googlebot, Bingbot and OAI-SearchBot are not blocked on the pages you want cited. Whether you allow training bots is a separate choice. Our robots.txt guide explains the file.

Step 2: Fix your firm facts

Write one set of facts: the legal name, address format, main phone number, offices, practice areas, and the names and roles of your lawyers. Make your website, Google Business Profile, bar and directory listings, and social profiles match. An AI tool that sees three versions of your address may repeat the wrong one, or skip you.

Step 3: Build practice pages that answer the question first

For each practice area, answer what a client actually asks: who you represent, what the process looks like in your state, how long it usually takes, how fees work and what to do now. Put a direct answer in the first sentence under each heading, then add detail and exceptions. Name the state or country the answer covers, and the date it was checked. Law changes, and old advice can mislead.

Step 4: Show who stands behind the content

Give each lawyer a real bio page with bar admissions, courts, practice areas, education and published work. Link each practice page to the lawyers who handle that work, and add a named author or reviewer to legal articles. Google's guidance for generative AI features says unique, expert-led content with a clear point of view matters more over time than tricks. Use LegalService markup for the firm and Person markup for each lawyer if you like. Google says special markup is not required for its AI features, but accurate markup can make the facts clearer.

Step 5: Cite your sources

Link to the statute, rule, court decision or government page that supports each factual claim, and quote the key text where it helps. This matches the tactic with the best test evidence, and it makes your page more useful to a reader who wants to check it.

Step 6: Keep your profiles and reviews honest and current

AI answers often draw on third-party sources, not only your site. Common ones for lawyers include your state bar's public directory, legal directories, Google reviews and local press. Run your prompts, list the sites the answers cite, and claim and complete the profiles that matter. Ask real clients for honest reviews, and follow your bar's rules on how you ask. Never post fake reviews.

Step 7: Build local pages only where they are real

Create a city or county page only where you have an office or regularly handle matters, and make it useful: local courts, procedures, how to meet you. Google warns that making many pages to manipulate rankings or AI answers breaks its scaled content abuse policy, and pages that only swap a city name add risk and no value.

Step 8: Measure monthly

Write 15 to 25 prompts your clients would use, split by practice area and city. Run each three times in ChatGPT, Google and Perplexity, and record whether your firm is named, whether your site is linked, which sources the answers cite and which competitors appear. Use Search Console's Generative AI performance report for Google impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode. What should a practice page contain?

A practice page works for people and for AI tools when each part answers one clear question. Use this outline and adapt it to your practice area and your state.

Opening answerWhat this area of law covers and whom you helpOne or two plain sentences, then detail
How the process worksThe usual steps and who is involvedName the state, court or agency and the date you checked
Time limits and key rulesDeadlines and rules that matterCite the statute or rule, and say when it changed if it did

The fee question matters. Marketers who work with small firms say one of the first questions a prospective client asks an AI tool is what a lawyer will cost, and a page that answers it has a better chance of being cited. That is a practitioner view, not a measured result. Answer it only as far as your rules and your practice allow.

What do clients ask AI about lawyers?

Clients ask two kinds of questions, and each needs a different page.

  • Information questions: "how long does probate take", "what happens at a first DUI hearing", "can my landlord keep my deposit". These are answered by practice pages and plain guides. Be careful to give general information and to say it is not legal advice.
  • Shopping questions: "best personal injury lawyer in my city", "what to ask a divorce lawyer before hiring". These are answered by third-party sources, reviews and lists, and by your own pages on how to choose a lawyer. Write that page honestly, with questions a client should ask any firm, and include what you do.

Clio found that many consumers asked AI how to find "the best" lawyer. If an AI tool typically lists key questions to ask, prepare strong, honest answers to each on your site.

How is GEO different from local SEO for law firms?

Local SEO aims to put your firm in the map pack and local results. It rests on your Google Business Profile, reviews, consistent name, address and phone number, and local pages. GEO builds on those same facts, and adds work on how AI tools describe you: answer-first pages, lawyer credentials, sourced claims and the third-party sites that AI answers cite. If your local SEO is weak, fix it first, because many AI answers about local services rely on the same listings. Our local SEO service covers that base.

Does GEO work for every practice area?

The method is the same, but the risks and the questions differ. Personal injury, family, immigration, criminal defense and estate planning all attract AI questions, and each has its own rules on advertising, results and confidentiality. Practice areas with strict rules on solicitation or testimonials need more care. Competition also differs by city and by area, so test your own prompts to see which firms and sources the tools name before you plan content.

What about Bing, ChatGPT and attorney markup?

OpenAI says ChatGPT search sometimes partners with other search providers, and its notes name Microsoft, so check that your key pages are indexed in Bing as well as Google. Bing Webmaster Tools lets you submit a sitemap and see AI citations in its AI Performance report. For markup, one law-firm guide recommends LegalService for the firm and Person for each lawyer, with sameAs links to the lawyer's state bar profile and legal directories, kept consistent with what the page shows. Google says no special markup is required for its AI features, so treat markup as a way to state facts clearly and not as a trick. A short audit for a firm is to check four things: crawler access, Bing and Google indexing, matching facts across the site and profiles, and a bio page for every lawyer with admissions and focus areas.

Worked example: a six-lawyer family law firm

This is an illustration, not a client result. Imagine a firm with offices in two cities. Its site has one page called "Family Law" with 300 words, no author, and an address that differs on its site and its directory listings.

The team runs 18 prompts three times in three tools, 162 answers in all. The firm is named in 11, about 7%. The answers cite two directories and a local news site where the firm is not listed, and one competitor's guide to custody rules.

The plan has five parts. The firm fixes its address everywhere. It splits the family page into five pages, such as divorce process and custody, each with an answer-first opening, the state statute cited and a reviewer byline. It adds bios for all six lawyers. It completes its two directory profiles, and asks former clients for honest reviews in line with its bar's rules. After 60 days it repeats the same 162 runs. Whatever the result, the firm keeps the sheet. The aim is a fair comparison, not a promise of leads.

How do you choose a GEO agency for a law firm?

Choose an agency that knows legal advertising rules and says so in writing. Ask who will review claims for compliance, how past results and testimonials are handled, and whether a lawyer at your firm approves each page. Ask for the prompt list, the raw answers, a source map and a ranked fix list. Be wary of any promise of a place in AI answers, because the AI companies do not publish how they choose. What are the common mistakes?

  • Thin practice pages. One short page for each practice area gives a model little to quote.
  • Unreviewed AI content. A lawyer must check every page for accuracy and for advertising compliance.
  • Inconsistent firm facts. Different addresses and names across sites cause confusion.
  • City pages with swapped names. They add risk and no value.
  • Chasing a rank in AI answers. The order changes from run to run.
  • Ignoring third-party sources. The answer may rely on a directory you have not claimed.
  • Skipping the rules. Superlatives, guarantees and unclear results can breach your bar's rules.
