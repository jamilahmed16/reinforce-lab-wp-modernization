Digital marketing strategies are plans for how a business will reach its goals using digital marketing channels. It says who your target audience is, where you will reach them, what you will say, how much you will spend and how you will know it worked.

This guide explains what digital marketing strategies are, how it differs from a campaign, how to build one in seven steps, and ten strategies you can choose from. It also shows how to measure results and which mistakes to avoid.

What is a digital marketing strategy?

Digital marketing strategies come in many forms, but they share one job: to turn online attention into customers.

A digital marketing strategy is a long-term plan. It connects your business goals to the channels and content you will use to win customers online. Semrush describes it as a plan for how a business will grow visibility and win its audience on digital channels. HubSpot adds that a good plan ties those choices to a budget, clear owners and measures of progress.

Many businesses skip this step. They have tactics but no digital marketing strategy behind them. They post on social media, run a few ads and hope. A strategy replaces that guesswork with choices you can explain and check.

What is the difference between a strategy, a campaign and a tactic?

People mix these three words up. They are different layers of the same plan.

LayerWhat it isExample
StrategyThe long-term plan and the reason behind itWin small accounting firms through search content and product demos
CampaignA time-limited push toward one goalA six-week workshop promotion aimed at 50 sign-ups
TacticOne action inside a campaignSend an email invitation or publish a guide

Changing an ad headline is a tactic. Changing who you sell to is a change of strategy. Strategies are usually set every quarter or every six months and checked every month.

Online marketing, internet marketing and digital marketing: are they different?

People use these three phrases to mean almost the same thing. Online marketing strategies, internet marketing strategies and digital marketing strategies all describe plans for reaching customers through the internet and digital tools. "Digital marketing" is the wider term and also covers things such as text messages and app messages. You will see all three in articles and agency pages. The steps in this guide apply to each of them.

Why do you need a digital marketing strategy?

Your potential customers search, compare and read reviews online before they ever contact you. Brand awareness starts there, and so do sales. If you are not there, they will find someone else. A written strategy helps in four ways:

  • It gives your team direction. Everyone knows which goal each task serves.
  • It stops random work. You can say no to channels that do not fit your buyers.
  • It makes spending easier to defend. Each channel has a goal and a number to beat.
  • It builds customer understanding. You write down who buys, why, and what stops them.

The numbers show where buyers and budgets are. DataReportal, using Kepios analysis, reported 6.04 billion internet users worldwide in October 2025, which is 73.2% of the population, while about 2.2 billion people were still offline. On the spending side, the Gartner 2025 CMO Spend Survey of 402 marketing leaders in North America, the UK and Europe found that digital channels made up 61.1% of total marketing spend. Gartner's 2026 survey of 401 leaders reported that digital media was more than two-thirds of media investment. Most respondents ran companies with more than $1 billion in revenue, so small businesses will spend differently.

What does a digital marketing strategy include?

Every strategy rests on the same six parts. Decide them in this order, because each one shapes the next.

  1. Audience. Who buys, what makes them buy and what holds them back. For business sales, separate the person who uses the product from the person who approves the purchase.
  2. Goals. The result everything points to, such as 20 percent more sign-ups next quarter.
  3. Channels. The marketing channels where you will meet the audience: search, social media, email, ads and so on.
  4. Budget and people. Money, tools and hours for each channel, and one named owner for each.
  5. Content and offer. The questions you will answer and the action each piece asks for.
  6. Measures. What you will track to see whether it works, both early signals and final results.

How to create a digital marketing strategy in 7 steps

1. Describe your customer

Start with real people, not guesses. Look at your recent wins and losses. Write down who they are, what they wanted, what almost stopped them and where they looked for answers. Talk to a few customers if you can. This is your customer profile, sometimes called a persona.

2. Set goals with a number and a date

A goal such as "more awareness" cannot be checked. A better one is "grow qualified enquiries by 20 percent in the next quarter". A good goal has an outcome, a target and a deadline. This is the idea behind SMART goals.

3. Check what you already do

List every channel you use now. Next to each one, note what it costs and what it brings in. Be honest about the ones you cannot measure. Keep what works, fix what almost works, and stop what does not.

4. Choose a few channels

Pick channels by where your buyers spend time, not by what is fashionable. Two or three done well beat seven done badly. The ten strategies below can help you choose.

5. Plan content around buyer questions

For each channel, list the questions your buyers ask and the next step you want them to take. Make sure your website lets them take it on a phone, whether that is a form, a call or a checkout.

6. Set the budget and name the owners

Cover media spend, tools, production and staff time. Give each channel one owner and a realistic number of hours a month. Allow time for design and approvals before you promise dates.

7. Measure, then adjust

Review your numbers every month. Keep a short written note of what you changed and why. Do not judge search work too fast. Google says some changes show in hours and others take months, and suggests waiting a few weeks before deciding whether a change helped.

10 digital marketing strategies to choose from

These are the main choices, and they are the different online marketing strategies most businesses use. You do not need all of them. Pick the ones that match your buyers and goals. Each one can be run as a marketing campaign inside your wider plan.

1. Search engine optimization (SEO)

SEO helps search engines find and understand your pages, so people can find you when they search. It covers your page content, your site's technical health and the trust other sites show in you. Results build over time and keep working after the work is done. Google is clear that there are no secrets that put a site first automatically.

2. Content marketing

Content marketing means publishing useful articles, guides and videos that answer buyer questions. Blog posts, guides and customer stories (user-generated content) all count. Good content turns a business from a seller into a trusted source. Google asks whether content gives original information and clear sourcing, and whether an expert wrote or reviewed it. Use those questions as your checklist.

3. Pay-per-click advertising (PPC)

PPC means you pay each time someone clicks your ad. It puts you in front of buyers fast, which SEO cannot do. The ad, the page it leads to and your follow-up must say the same thing. Track which clicks become customers, not only how many clicks you get.

4. Social media marketing

Social media works best when you pick the platforms your buyers use and post things people want to share or reply to. A business buyer may be on LinkedIn. A shop selling to young people may do better on short video. Do not try to be everywhere.

5. Email marketing

Email lets you speak to people who already know you. Gartner's 2025 survey ranked email the fifth most impactful channel overall and the top channel for customer loyalty, even though it took only 7.4% of digital spend. Use it to welcome new contacts, send useful tips and remind buyers who left something behind. Ask permission first and make it easy to unsubscribe. A small list of the right people is worth more than a large list of strangers.

You will often see a claim that email returns $36 for every $1. Treat it with care. The UK Data and Marketing Association's 2026 Marketer Email Tracker, a survey of 250 marketers, put the figure at £40.96 per £1, up from £38.33 in 2021. These are marketers' own estimates, not measured revenue, so use them as a sign that email is worth testing and measure your own return.

6. Influencer marketing

Influencer marketing means working with people your audience already trusts. Choose people whose followers match your buyers, even if their audience is small. Agree what you will pay for and how you will measure it before you start.

7. Affiliate marketing

In affiliate marketing, partners promote your product and earn a fee for each sale or lead they bring. You only pay when it works. Choose partners carefully, because they speak for your brand. Ask partners to say clearly that they earn a fee.

8. Mobile marketing

Many buyers use a phone first. Mobile marketing means making your site fast and easy to use on a small screen, and using text messages or app messages where they fit. Good user experience on a phone matters most. Test your main page and your form on a real phone before you spend a cent on ads.

9. Video marketing

Video shows how a product works and lets people meet your team. Short videos suit social media. Longer ones suit your website and YouTube. Add captions so people can watch without sound.

10. Search optimized for AI and voice

People now ask questions out loud and in AI tools such as ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI answers. To be named in those answers, write clear answers to real questions, show who wrote the page, and keep your facts the same everywhere. Semrush notes that the same authority and structure that earn search rankings also shape which sites AI tools cite, so good SEO helps here too.

Which digital marketing channel is the most effective?

No channel wins for every business, but the Gartner 2025 CMO Spend Survey gives a useful picture from 402 marketing leaders. Search advertising stood out as a high-spend, high-impact channel, particularly near the point of purchase. Video and streaming slipped in impact rankings as platforms became crowded. Email ranked fifth for impact overall and first for customer loyalty. Most of those leaders ran large companies, so use the ranking as a prompt to test, not as a rule for a small business.

Match the channel to the job. Use search and ads to capture people who are ready to buy, content and SEO to build steady demand, and email to keep customers and bring them back.

What about positioning, messaging and tools?

Two parts of a strategy are easy to skip. The first is positioning and messaging: one clear sentence on who you help, what you do for them and why you are different. Every channel should repeat it. A strategy with ten channels and three different messages confuses buyers.

The second is tools and workflow. Decide where your contacts, content and results live, and who owns each task. A shared sheet and a simple calendar are enough for a small team. Add a tool only when a manual step starts to cost you time or leads.

How often should you update your strategy?

Review results monthly and rework the plan every quarter. A monthly look catches a channel that has stopped working. A quarterly rework lets you move budget, drop weak tactics and set the next goal. Change one thing at a time where you can, so you know what caused a result.

A worked example

This example is made up to show how the pieces fit. It is not a real result.

A company sells scheduling software to small clinics. Its goal is to grow demo requests by 20 percent in one quarter.

  • Audience: clinic managers who lose time to phone bookings.
  • Channels: search (SEO) and email. Social media is left out for now.
  • Content: three guides that answer the questions managers search for, each ending with a demo request.
  • Budget: one writer for 20 hours a month and a small tool budget.
  • Measures: guide visits and email sign-ups early on, demo requests as the final result.

Every part points to the same goal. If demo requests do not rise, the team can see which step to change.

Digital marketing strategies examples: three online marketing strategy examples

These examples are made up to show how digital marketing strategies can differ from one business to another. They are not real results.

A local service business

A plumber wants more calls in one town. The audience is homeowners with an urgent problem. The strategy is local search and reviews: a clear Google Business Profile, a page for each service and area, and a steady way to ask happy customers for reviews. The key number is calls per month.

A business software company

A company sells project software to small agencies. Buyers compare tools for weeks. The strategy is content and email: guides that answer their questions, comparison pages, and a short email series after they download a guide. The key number is demo requests.

An online shop

A shop sells outdoor gear. Customers browse on phones and buy often. The strategy is search for product pages, email for repeat buyers and short videos that show the products in use. The key numbers are sales and repeat orders.

The strategies are different because the customers behave differently. Copying a competitor's plan without checking who your buyers are is a common way to waste money.

What is a good digital marketing strategy for a small business?

A small business has less money and fewer people, so focus matters more. A good small business plan looks like this:

  • Choose one main goal. For example, more qualified enquiries.
  • Start with the channels you can run well. One person can usually run search content and email, or one social platform and a few ads.
  • Make your website do the selling. Clear pages, a fast site and a simple form matter more than more traffic.
  • Ask for reviews and referrals. They are cheap and trusted.
  • Keep a simple tracker. One page with your goal, channels and monthly numbers is enough.

Large companies add more channels, more tools and more people. The steps are the same.

How much should you spend on digital marketing?

There is no single right amount, but published benchmarks give you a starting point:

  • Large companies. Gartner's 2025 survey found marketing budgets at 7.7% of company revenue, flat on the year before. Its 2026 article puts the average at 7.8%. Paid media took 30.6% of budgets in the 2025 survey. These are firms with mostly over $1 billion in revenue.
  • Small businesses. The US Small Business Administration's Ascent marketing plan worksheet is widely quoted as suggesting 7 to 8 percent of revenue for firms under $5 million in revenue with profit margins of 10 to 12 percent, and it says budgets can range from 2 percent to 20 percent of sales. We could not open the SBA file ourselves, so we cite it as quoted by boomp.net, which read it in October 2026. With thinner margins, start lower.
  • A split rule. Xero's small business guide suggests the 70/20/10 rule: 70% on proven tactics, 20% on promising ideas and 10% on experiments.

Work out your own number like this:

  1. Decide how many new customers you want.
  2. Estimate how many leads you need to win that many, using your own past numbers.
  3. Test a small budget on one or two channels and note the cost per lead.
  4. Put more money into the channel that brings leads at a price you can afford, and cut the one that does not.

Count staff time and tools as well as ad spend. Gartner's 2026 survey found that labor's share of marketing budgets rose from 21.9% to 24.5%, which suggests that people and skills cost real money even when tools are cheap.

How long does it take to see results?

It depends on the channel. Paid ads can send visitors within days, while search and content work builds over months. We found no reliable public study that gives one time frame for all businesses, so we do not quote one. Xero's guide suggests testing channels for the first three months and moving budget away from a campaign that has not improved after four to six weeks of changes. That is advice, not a measured rule, but it gives you a sensible rhythm to start with.

Should you do it yourself or hire help?

Do the planning yourself, even if you hire people to run channels. Only you know your customers, your margins and your goals. Hire help when a channel needs skills or time you do not have, such as technical SEO, paid ad management or video. Ask any provider what they will change, how they will measure it and what you can check yourself. Start with free or low-cost work, such as a complete Google Business Profile and your own site pages, before you pay for ads.

A 90-day plan to put your strategy into action

  • Days 1 to 30: set up. Describe your customer, set the goal, check your current channels and fix your website basics. Set up tracking so you can see where enquiries come from.
  • Days 31 to 60: launch. Publish your first content, start your chosen channels and send your first emails. Record what you did.
  • Days 61 to 90: learn and adjust. Compare your numbers with your goal. Keep what works, fix what almost works and stop what does not. Write the plan for the next quarter.

How do you measure a digital marketing strategy?

Track two kinds of numbers. Early signals, such as visits, sign-ups and replies, show whether you are moving. Final results, such as enquiries, sales and revenue, show whether it pays.

Use Google Search Console to see how your pages perform in Google. Use your analytics tool to see what visitors do on your site. Set up your goals before you start, so you can compare before and after. Review monthly and keep a short log of what you changed.

What are the most common mistakes?

  • Starting with channels, not customers. Follow the audience, not the trend.
  • Having no number to beat. If you cannot measure it, you cannot improve it.
  • Doing too much at once. Spread thin, every channel gets weak results.
  • Chasing followers, not buyers. Likes from the wrong people do not pay.
  • Judging too soon. Search and content take weeks to show effect.
  • Never reviewing. A plan written once and never checked goes out of date.

Wrapping up

The best digital marketing strategies are simple. Know your customer. Set a goal with a number. Choose a few channels. Name an owner for each. Measure and adjust every month.

Strong digital marketing strategies tie every channel to one goal.
