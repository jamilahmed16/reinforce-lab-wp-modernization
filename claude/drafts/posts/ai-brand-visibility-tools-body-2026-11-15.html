An AI brand visibility tool shows how often AI answers name, describe or link to your brand, and how that compares with your competitors. It runs a fixed set of buyer questions through tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews, then records the answers. A good one shows the full answer, the sources behind it and the trend over time. A weak one gives a single score you cannot check.

This guide is for B2B marketing teams that are deciding whether to buy one. It explains what these tools track, what they cannot do, what the entry plans of two well-known tools cost, and a checklist for choosing. It includes two worked examples and a 14-day test you can run before you sign anything. If you want to try the method by hand first, start with our 30-minute AI brand visibility test.

What is an AI brand visibility tool?

An AI brand visibility tool is software that asks AI search tools a list of questions on a schedule and records whether your brand appears in the answers. The software owner chooses the prompts, the engines and the competitors. The tool then reports results such as mention rate, citations, share of voice and sentiment.

You will see other names for the same thing: AI visibility platform, AI search visibility tool, GEO tool, AEO tool, LLM tracking tool and AI brand monitoring. The names overlap. What matters is what the product records and how you can check it.

Two versions exist. The first is a simple monitor that reports where you appear. The second adds advice, such as which page to change or which site to approach. Decide early which one you need. A monitor is enough to find out if you have a problem. You need more once you know what to fix and who will fix it.

What do these tools track?

Most tools report the same core measures. Ahrefs lists mentions, citations, impressions and AI share of voice in its AI visibility audit guide. Semrush lists an AI visibility score, mentions, citations, platform coverage, top cited pages, topics and a competitor comparison for its free checker. Here are the measures in plain words:

MeasureThe question it answersWatch out for
MentionsIn what share of answers is my brand named?A mention is not a link. Check that the tool counts them separately.
CitationsIn what share of answers does the tool link to a page of mine?Ask to see the exact URL for each one.
Share of voiceOf all brand mentions in my test, what share is mine?Each vendor calculates it differently. Ask for the formula.
Sources citedWhich pages do the tools cite in my market?This list is often the most useful output, because it shows where to earn mentions.
SentimentIs the brand described in a good, neutral or bad way?Read a sample of the answers yourself. Automated scoring can miss tone.
AccuracyAre the facts about us right?Most tools do not check this for you. Plan to check by hand.
HistoryIs the trend going up or down?Ask how long results are kept.

Some tools also report a "position" or "rank" inside an answer. Be careful with it. See the next section.

What can these tools not do?

AI answers are not like search results, so no tool can offer the certainty a rank tracker gives. Four limits matter most.

Answers change from run to run

SparkToro and Gumshoe ran a study in January 2026 with about 600 volunteers and 12 prompts across ChatGPT, Claude and Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode. They found less than a 1 in 100 chance of getting the same list of brands twice for the same prompt, and less than a 1 in 1,000 chance of the same order. Their conclusion was that a "ranking position in AI" figure is not meaningful, while the share of runs in which you appear is a reasonable measure. A good tool therefore repeats each prompt and reports a rate, not a single screenshot.

No tool sees inside Google

Google says that no third-party tool has access to its internal ranking or AI systems, and it warns about tools that claim to use internal Google metrics. For its own AI features, Google points to the Generative AI performance report in Search Console. That report is free, so use it as your first Google signal.

A score is a snapshot

Answers vary by prompt wording, country, model version and date. A score from one day tells you little. Repeated measurements tell you whether a change lasts.

Tools do not fix anything on their own

A dashboard shows a gap. Someone still has to write the page, correct the facts or earn the mention. Decide who owns that work before you buy. The software is the cheap part.

Why do B2B teams track AI visibility at all?

Because buyers use AI tools, and surveys show it, with a catch: each survey asks a different question, so the numbers are not interchangeable. Here are three, with what each one actually measured.

  • Forrester: 94% of business buyers report using AI somewhere in the buying process, up from 89% a year earlier (Buyers' Journey Survey 2025, published January 2026). That counts any use at any stage.
  • Gartner: in a survey of 645 B2B buyers in August and September 2025, 45% said they used generative AI during a recent purchase, mainly to gather information on vendors and products. Buyers used an average of seven information sources. 69% preferred to validate AI-generated insights with a sales rep.
  • 6sense: its 2026 report, based on 4,611 recent B2B buyers, found that 81% use an LLM somewhere in an evaluation, down from 94% the year before. Only 26% use an LLM to discover vendors. Before contacting a seller, 21% said LLMs influenced their view of vendors, against 34% who cited their own existing knowledge and 32% who cited internal discussions.

Read together, they say that most buyers touch AI, a smaller share use it to find new vendors, and it often sorts and checks what buyers already know. 6sense also found that buyers chose the vendor they favored before contacting a seller 82% of the time, and that 92% of eventual winners were on the initial shortlist. That is why being named in AI answers matters before the first sales call: it helps get you onto the shortlist and keeps you on it. Treat the figures as a reason to measure, not as proof that AI answers will win deals for you.

Are AI visibility tools, trackers and monitoring tools the same thing?

Mostly yes. Vendors use different labels for the same job: AI brand visibility tool, AI search visibility tool, AI visibility tracker, AI brand monitoring, LLM tracking tool and GEO tool. Each one runs a set of prompts in AI tools such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews. It records whether your brand appears, and it reports mentions, citations, share of voice and sentiment.

The real differences are which AI tools it covers, how often it runs your prompts, how it handles competitor benchmarking and what reporting you get. Several SEO platforms, including Semrush and Ahrefs, list AI visibility features too. Ask whether you need a separate tracker at all.

How do you choose one? A 10-point checklist

Comparison guides use similar criteria. These ten are our version. The first five decide whether the data is useful. The last five decide whether you can use it.

  1. Engines. Does it cover the tools your buyers use, on the plan you would buy? A vendor may list an engine that your plan does not include.
  2. Google AI Mode and AI Overviews. Are both covered? Check the plan.
  3. Mentions and citations kept apart. A brand named without a link and a link without a name are different events.
  4. The full answer. Can you read the whole answer and every source, not only a score?
  5. History per prompt. Can you see how one prompt changed over weeks?
  6. Prompt control. Can you add your own buyer questions, or are you stuck with the vendor's list?
  7. Competitors. Can you track three to five named rivals for the same prompts?
  8. Exports and alerts. Can you export the data and get a warning when a prompt that was working drops?
  9. Price per prompt and trial. Is there a trial, and what happens when you pass the prompt limit?
  10. Who acts on it. Does the tool point to the page or source to fix, and do you have a person to do it?

Ask every vendor the same questions and write the answers in one sheet. If a vendor will not show a full answer with its sources, treat that as a warning.

Worked example 1: a B2B software company runs a 14-day test

This is an illustration, not a client result. Imagine a company that sells scheduling software to clinics. It wants to know if it needs a paid tool. It writes 25 buyer prompts: 10 for the category, 8 for comparisons and 7 for brand questions. It names three competitors.

On day 1 it runs the 25 prompts by hand in three AI tools and saves the answers. It is named in 4 of 75 answers, which is about 5%. It tries Google Search Console and sees AI feature data for its pages.

On days 2 to 7 it tests two trial accounts with the same 25 prompts. It scores each tool from 0 to 2 on the ten points above. Tool A scores 16 of 20 and shows full answers, sources and history. Tool B scores 11 of 20. It gives a single visibility score, does not show full answers, and counts a mention and a link as one event.

On days 8 to 14 it compares the tools' numbers against its hand checks. Tool A matches within a few answers. Tool B reports a mention rate that is nearly twice what the team saw by hand. The team picks Tool A on the entry plan, because it can read every answer.

The company does not treat the tool as the end of the job. It lists the five pages most often cited for its category, a review site and four blogs, and plans one real story for two of them.

Worked example 2: a small consultancy decides it does not need a paid tool

Again, this is an illustration. Imagine a three-person consultancy that sells compliance training. It has 12 buyer questions and no budget for software.

It tests the 12 questions monthly in three AI tools by hand, which takes about an hour. It also runs the free Ahrefs and Semrush checkers as a second opinion and watches the Search Console AI report. For a paid tool at $95 a month, it would get 50 prompts, so it would use under a quarter of the allowance. After three months the team sees that its results are stable, and that the work it needs is writing and outreach, not more data.

The lesson is that the right tool depends on the size of your prompt list and your team. A spreadsheet can be enough when the list is short, and software saves time when the list is long or covers several markets.

How do you run a 14-day proof-of-value test?

Use this before you sign a contract, especially an annual one.

  1. Days 1 to 2: write the prompts. Write 20 to 30 real buyer questions without your brand name. Add two or three competitors and your country.
  2. Days 2 to 3: run a hand baseline. Ask 5 to 10 of the prompts yourself in each engine and save the answers.
  3. Days 3 to 10: run the trials. Use the same prompts and competitors in every trial.
  4. Days 10 to 12: check the data. Compare each tool with your hand baseline. Open ten answers and confirm that the brand and sources shown are real.
  5. Days 12 to 13: test the follow-up. Can the tool tell you what to fix, or only that there is a gap?
  6. Day 14: decide. Score each tool on the ten points. Count the real cost, including staff time.

Run the test over a period when nothing big changes on your site, so you do not mix tool noise with real change.

How do you monitor brand mentions in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity?

People search for this in many ways: track brand mentions in ChatGPT, AI brand monitoring, LLM brand monitoring, or tools for monitoring brand visibility in AI interfaces. They all describe one job. You pick a fixed set of questions your buyers ask, run them in each AI tool on a schedule, and record what each answer says about you.

Five things matter when you monitor:

  • Mentions: does your brand name appear in the answer?
  • Citations: does the answer link to your site or to a page that names you?
  • Accuracy: are the facts about your prices, services and locations right?
  • Sentiment: is the wording about you positive, neutral or negative?
  • Competitors: who else is named for the same question?

Check which AI tools a vendor covers. Common ones include ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, Grok, Microsoft Copilot and Google AI Overviews, and coverage differs by vendor. A paid tool automates the running and the logging. It does not change what the AI tools say. If you want a Perplexity brand mention monitoring tool, a tool that watches ChatGPT and Gemini together, or a tool for AI Mode, check each vendor's list of covered AI tools and how often it runs your prompts. Coverage differs.

How do you track brand mentions in ChatGPT without a paid tool?

You can run a simple test by hand. It is slower than a tool, but it shows you whether paying for one is worth it.

  1. List 15 to 25 buyer prompts. Use the questions your customers ask, such as "best [your category] for [your buyer]."
  2. Run each prompt in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Mode. Use a new chat each time.
  3. Record the result in a sheet: prompt, tool, date, whether your brand appeared, its position, the sources cited, the competitors named and whether the facts were right.
  4. Repeat weekly at the same time with the same prompts.
  5. Read trends, not single answers. Answers change by person, place and time, so treat each run as a sample.

If the sheet shows you rarely appear, fix the pages and facts the answers rely on first. A tool will only measure the same problem faster.

Why should you track AI brand visibility?

Because buyers now ask AI tools to shortlist vendors, and you cannot see those answers in a normal rank report. Tracking tells you whether you are named, whether the facts are right and who is named instead of you. Without it you are guessing. Tracking does not prove the answers cause sales, so treat it as one input next to leads and revenue.

What should you do after the tool finds a gap?

  1. Fix wrong facts first. A wrong price or service description costs more than a missed mention.
  2. Read the cited pages. The pages that AI tools cite for your category show where your competitors are mentioned and you are not.
  3. Add direct answers. Put a clear answer, a named source and a date near the top of your key pages.
  4. Earn mentions where tools already look. Pitch real stories and real data to those sites.
  5. Repeat the test monthly and report a rate with a range, not one number.

What else do B2B teams ask?

How are these tools different from SEO tools?

A rank tracker records the position of your page in a list of results. An AI visibility tool records whether your brand appears inside a written answer, with which sources. The two answer different questions, and you need both. Ahrefs describes GEO as building on SEO, and Google says its AI features rest on the same ranking and quality systems as Search. Strong SEO still helps you get retrieved. The AI tool then shows whether you were named.

Can an SEO suite do the job?

Often, for a first look. Ahrefs and Semrush both offer AI visibility features and free checkers. A stand-alone tracker may offer more prompt control and history. Compare what each one shows against the ten points above rather than assuming that a bigger suite is better.

Should you use more than one tool?

One tool with the right engines is usually enough. Use your hand test as a second opinion. Ahrefs found that the most cited brands differ between Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT and Perplexity, so check that your one tool covers the engines your buyers use rather than adding a second tool.

How often should you sample?

There is no standard. SparkToro's study found that a brand shows up across many runs of the same prompt even when the list changes each time, so the more runs you have, the better the rate. A tool can run daily. By hand, monthly is a sensible minimum. Report an average and a range, and do not react to one day.

Do these tools show what real people ask?

No. They run the prompts that you or the vendor chose. AI platforms do not share what users ask, so there is no public prompt volume like the search volume in keyword tools. Search Engine Land makes this point in its guide to measuring GEO. Build your prompt list from sales calls, support emails and the questions prospects send you.

Can a tool prove revenue impact?

Not on its own. A tool shows that you appeared in answers. It cannot see the private conversation, so it cannot show who read the answer or what they did next. To estimate impact, combine the tool with referral visits from AI tool domains in your analytics, a "how did you hear about us" field on forms, and your sales team's notes. Treat the link to revenue as a clue and not as proof.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Buying before testing by hand. You cannot judge a tool's data without a baseline.
  • Trusting one score. Keep mentions, citations and accuracy separate.
  • Chasing a rank in AI answers. The order in answers is close to random.
  • Buying the cheapest plan without checking the limits. Prompts, engines and markets decide what you can learn.
  • Tracking only branded questions. You will look better than you are. Include category and comparison questions.
  • Treating the tool as the program. The person who acts on the results matters more than the software.
  • Using a vendor's own "best tool" list as proof. Many such lists are written by vendors.
