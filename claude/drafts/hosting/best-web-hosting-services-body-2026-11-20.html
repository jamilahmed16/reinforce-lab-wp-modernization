What is the best web hosting for beginners?

For a first WordPress site, Bluehost has the guided setup that TechRadar praises, and it is recommended by WordPress.org, according to Bluehost. Hostinger is the best low-price start and its tools are simple. Namecheap keeps your domain, hosting and email in one account. All three list a 30-day money-back guarantee, so you can test them. Read our Bluehost review first if you want the most guidance.

What is the best web hosting for small business?

A small business site needs email, backups and support it can reach. Namecheap's shared plans include mailboxes, and Nexcess includes email hosting in every plan. Bluehost adds phone support on its Business plan. DreamHost allows 25 sites on its cheapest plan. Hostinger Unlimited adds daily backups and staging. For a business site that earns money, Kinsta and Cloudways are managed options with human support.

What is the best web hosting for ecommerce?

For a store, speed at checkout and the right plan size matter more than the price. Nexcess runs WooCommerce, though Webhostingcat saw a slow checkout on Liquid Web in one test. Kinsta recommends Single 65k or larger for stores. Cloudways suits stores on a 4 GB server or larger. Bluehost sells an eCommerce Essentials plan, and Hostinger's Ecommerce store builder starts on its Unlimited plan. Hostinger is not PCI compliant, according to All About Cookies, so check how your payment provider handles card data.

Shared, VPS or dedicated hosting: which do you need?

Finding the Universe, which has hosted three sites on Cloudways since 2019, gives a simple guide. Shared hosting suits sites under about 10,000 visitors a month. A VPS suits sites above that, up to well over a million visitors depending on the design. Dedicated servers suit very large sites. Most of the hosts above sell shared plans first and let you move up later on the same account. Cloud hosting sits between shared hosting and a VPS. How do you choose the best web hosting service?

  1. Start with the job. A blog, a business site and a store need different things.
  2. Write down the renewal price and date. Use the tables above.
  3. Check where your visitors are. Several hosts are fast near the US and slower in Asia without a CDN.
  4. Pick the support you need. Phone support is rare, and chat support varies.
  5. Use the money-back window. Test speed and support on your own site in the first 30 days.

What should you check before you buy hosting?

  • The renewal price and date. Write both down before you pay.
  • The term. The lowest price often needs a 12, 36 or 48-month term paid up front.
  • The money-back window. It ranges from 30 days to 90 days. Use it to test speed and support.
  • Support channels. Phone support is rare. Network Solutions and InMotion list it, and Bluehost adds it on Business. Hostinger, Kinsta and DreamHost do not.
  • Where your visitors are. Several hosts are fast near the US and slower in Asia without a CDN.
  • Card payments. Hostinger is not PCI compliant, according to All About Cookies. If you take cards on your own site, check how your payment provider handles it.
