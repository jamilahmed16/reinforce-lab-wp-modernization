What did we find? The short version

  • Speed and uptime are strong. Two independent tests measured 100% uptime. All About Cookies recorded a 138 ms average response. Cybernews recorded 29 ms.
  • The sale price is real, and so is the renewal. Premium costs $1.99 a month on a 48-month term. It renews at $10.99 a month.
  • AI tools are included. You get an AI website builder, an AI assistant, AI credits and an MCP server for AI coding tools.
  • Security is basic. There is a firewall, malware scanning and DDoS protection. Hostinger is not PCI compliant, which matters for online stores.
  • Support is chat and email only. There is no phone line. Chat starts with an AI assistant and moves to a person when needed.

How secure is Hostinger?

All About Cookies lists these security features: a web application firewall, a malware scanner, DDoS protection through a Cloudflare DNS firewall, WHOIS privacy and a secure access manager. Cybernews adds free SSL and backups. That covers the basics for a blog or a small business site.

The gap is payments. All About Cookies notes that Hostinger's servers are not built to meet PCI compliance standards. If you take card payments on your own site, use a payment provider that handles the card step, or choose a host with a clear PCI path. IONOS and GoDaddy both offer one, according to All About Cookies.

What is Hostinger support like?

  • Channels: 24/7 live chat and email. There is no phone support.
  • How chat works: You start with the Kodee AI assistant. A person joins if the AI cannot solve the problem.
  • What testers found: Cybernews says support is good but less hands-on than it used to be. The team often pointed its reviewer to guides instead of fixing the problem.
  • What customers say: Cybernews reports a Trustpilot score in the 4.4 to 4.7 range, with mixed Reddit threads. People praise the price and complain about renewals and AI-led support.

If you need a person on the phone for an urgent problem, this is the weak spot.

Can Hostinger grow with your site?

Yes, for a while. You can start on shared hosting, move to Cloud Startup, then to a VPS. Hostinger's VPS plans have one-click templates for WordPress, Docker, n8n and OpenClaw. Cybernews installed an AI agent on a VPS in under 20 minutes this way. You get full root access, but you manage the server yourself. Hostinger has no dedicated servers, so very large sites will need to move to another host.

How do you get started? Six steps

  1. Pick a plan. Premium for one small site, Unlimited for several sites or daily backups, Cloud Startup for more traffic.
  2. Choose a term. The 48-month term gives the lowest price. A shorter term costs more per month but commits you for less time.
  3. Claim the free domain. It is free for the first year on all plans except monthly billing.
  4. Build or move your site. Start from the AI builder, install WordPress, or ask for the free migration of an existing site.
  5. Check backups and security. Premium has weekly backups. Make sure the firewall and malware scanner are on.
  6. Set a renewal reminder. Put the date in your calendar, with the new price, so the increase is never a surprise.

What mistakes should you avoid?

  • Budgeting for $1.99 a month forever. Budget for about $3.79 a month over five years, or $10.99 a month at renewal.
  • Taking card payments on the site without a payment provider that handles PCI. Hostinger is not PCI compliant.
  • Skipping the money-back window. You have 30 days to test speed, support and the builder.
  • Expecting phone support. Plan for chat and email.
  • Treating the AI credits as a monthly allowance. They are a one-time bonus that expires with the plan.

Five questions to ask before you buy

  1. Can I pay for the full term now, and do I still want this site in four years?
  2. Will I take card payments directly on this site?
  3. Do I need daily backups? If yes, skip Premium.
  4. Do I need to speak to a person on the phone?
  5. Have I written down the renewal price and date?
