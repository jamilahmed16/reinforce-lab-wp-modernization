What is Cloudways, and how is it different from traditional hosting?

Cloudways is managed cloud hosting. It sets up servers on cloud providers such as DigitalOcean, Vultr, Linode, AWS and Google Cloud, tunes them for WordPress and looks after them. You manage the site. Cloudways manages the server. Finding the Universe, which has hosted three sites on Cloudways since September 2019, says the platform hosts more than 570,000 websites for over 100,000 customers.

Traditional shared hosting puts many sites on one server and charges a fixed monthly price. Cloudways gives your site its own server resources and bills for the size you pick. You also get more control than on most managed hosts, such as choosing the provider, the PHP version and the data center. The trade-off is a steeper learning curve than a simple shared plan, which many reviews mention.

Is Cloudways beginner-friendly?

It is friendlier than running your own server, and harder than basic shared hosting. The dashboard is clean, and you can launch a WordPress site in a few clicks. You still choose a provider, a server size and a data center, and you may need to add a CDN or email service yourself. Several reviews call this a learning curve. If you want to set one up and forget it, a simpler host may suit you better. If you want growth room and a clear dashboard, Cloudways works well after a short learning period.

What costs extra on Cloudways?

The headline price covers the server. The reviews we read list these extras:

  • Backups. Finding the Universe says backups are not included in the headline price.
  • CDN and email. Hostingstep says a CDN and email are add-ons, so global reach and mailboxes cost more.
  • Bigger plans. Finding the Universe says the price climbs faster than renting a raw server once you reach larger plans.

DevOpsCube lists two more limits: no root access and no email hosting. Add these costs to your plan before you compare prices with a shared host.

Which Cloudways provider and plan should you pick?

Can Cloudways handle traffic spikes and online stores?

The Hostingstep load test sends a burst of visitors at the server. The DigitalOcean plans finished every request. The Vultr and Linode plans were quicker per request but dropped 1.5% to 2.2% of them at the peak. For a store where every checkout counts, a plan that completes every request is the safer pick. Three of the four reviews we read mention WooCommerce, and all describe Cloudways as a fit for stores that have outgrown shared hosting. If your store takes card payments, check how your payment provider handles the card step, and pick Medium or larger.

Can Cloudways grow with your site? Scalability explained

Yes. Finding the Universe says you can scale a server up in size, or even switch providers, with a few clicks. There is no limit on how many applications you run on one server, so you pay for the server and not per site. Autonomous plans scale on their own. Cloudways also runs applications other than WordPress, such as Magento, Joomla, Laravel and Drupal, which helps an agency that hosts more than one kind of site.

Does Cloudways use cPanel? What is the dashboard like?

No. Cloudways does not use cPanel, the control panel many shared hosts use. It has its own clean platform dashboard for servers, applications, backups and settings. Reviewers describe the interface as easy to read once you know the terms. Three of the four reviews also mention staging sites, where you test changes before you go live, and three mention Cloudflare, the usual route for a CDN. Check the plan page for what is included.

How do you move a site to Cloudways?

  1. Start the free trial and create a server on your chosen provider.
  2. Copy your site to the new server, or use a migration tool.
  3. Test the copy: pages, forms, checkout and speed.
  4. Lower your domain's DNS time, then point the domain at the new server.
  5. Keep the old host live for a few days, and watch rankings and errors.

Who should use Cloudways, and who should avoid it?

Use it if you run an established WordPress or WooCommerce site and want speed and control without server work. It also suits agencies that run several sites and want to scale each one.

Avoid it if you want the lowest price for a tiny site, a mailbox in the same account, or full control of every layer of the server. Finding the Universe says power users who want very large servers do better renting raw infrastructure directly.
