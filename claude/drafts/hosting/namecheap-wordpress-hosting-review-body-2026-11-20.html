Namecheap is best known for domains, and it sells two ways to host WordPress: shared hosting with cPanel, and EasyWP, its managed WordPress plan. The two work very differently, and the right one depends on how many sites you need. This page compares both, with prices, limits, test results and setup steps. Prices were read on Namecheap's own pages on 20 November 2026.

Is Namecheap hosting worth it? The short version

  • Cheap, with a smaller renewal jump than many hosts. Stellar is $2.28 a month on a yearly plan and renews at $55.88 a year, about $4.66 a month.
  • Two WordPress routes. Shared hosting hosts several sites and includes email. EasyWP hosts one site per plan and has no bundled email.
  • Speed depends on where your visitors are. Independent tests found fast responses in the US and slower ones in Asia.
  • Support is chat and help desk. Namecheap lists live chat and a 24/7 help desk.
  • The sale ends 4 December 2026. Namecheap lists a 30-day money-back guarantee.

What does EasyWP include?

EasyWP runs on Namecheap's cloud. According to Namecheap's help article, every site includes free Let's Encrypt SSL, built-in advanced caching, SFTP and phpMyAdmin access. WPThinker lists a CDN, automatic backups, WordPress updates and a web application firewall on every plan. HostingStep reported in its 2026 test that EasyWP Starter had no automatic backups. The sources disagree, so check the backup option in your dashboard before you rely on it.

WPThinker also lists EasyWP Turbo, with 50 GB, a free domain and business email, and EasyWP Supersonic, with 100 GB and priority support. If you are not sure, EasyWP Starter has a free first month on monthly billing, then $9.88 a month, according to WPThinker.

Is Namecheap good for WordPress?

Yes for small and medium sites, on either route. Shared hosting gives you cPanel and Softaculous to install WordPress, plus free SSL, unmetered bandwidth and an AI website builder, according to WPThinker. EasyWP gives you a simpler dashboard built for WordPress. The limits are speed outside the US and the fact that shared hosting runs on Apache. WebHostWatch notes that Apache hosting trails LiteSpeed hosts such as Hostinger on first-byte time.

Who should use Namecheap, and who should look elsewhere?

Namecheap suits: bloggers and small sites on a budget, people who want their domain and hosting in one account, and anyone who wants email included. Choose EasyWP for one managed WordPress site.

Look elsewhere if: most of your visitors are in Asia or far from its servers, you want LiteSpeed speed, or you need daily backups on a budget plan. Our best cloud hosting page covers managed cloud servers, and our Bluehost review shows another WordPress-focused option.

How do you build a WordPress site on Namecheap? Step by step

  1. Choose shared hosting or EasyWP. Several sites or email: shared hosting. One WordPress site: EasyWP.
  2. Choose a plan and a term. Check the renewal price in your cart. The sale ends 4 December 2026.
  3. Register or connect your domain. You can buy the domain from Namecheap in the same account, or point one you own.
  4. Install WordPress. On shared hosting, install it with Softaculous in cPanel. On EasyWP, WordPress is set up when you create the site.
  5. Check SSL. Both routes include free SSL.
  6. Pick a theme and add pages. Add a home page, an about page and a contact page first. Then set up SEO and security plugins.
  7. Set up backups and a renewal reminder. Check your backup option, and add the renewal date and price to your calendar.
