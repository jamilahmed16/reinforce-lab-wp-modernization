DigitalOcean is a cloud provider for developers. You rent a virtual server, called a Droplet, from $4 a month, and you run it yourself. It is not a hosting company that sets up and looks after your WordPress site. This DigitalOcean review reads DigitalOcean's own pricing and billing pages, five independent reports with real numbers and the top-ranking reviews. The pattern is clear. Prices are fair and steady, network speed is excellent, and WordPress speed depends on caching and on which Droplet you pick. Here are the details.

What did we find? The short version

  • Prices are simple and do not rise. A $4 Droplet stays $4. There is no intro discount and no renewal jump, according to WPThinker.
  • WordPress needs caching. A $6 Droplet took 1.66 seconds to first byte without a cache and 0.40 seconds with WP Super Cache in one test.
  • It is not managed. You handle updates, security and backups, and backups cost 20% to 30% extra.
  • Support is free by ticket. Faster support and chat sit on paid support plans.
  • New accounts get a $5 credit. It applies by itself for 90 days, and no code is needed.

What is the DigitalOcean uptime SLA?

DigitalOcean promises 99.99% monthly uptime for each Droplet, which allows about four minutes of downtime in a 30-day month. If it falls short, you can claim a credit of 100% of that Droplet's charges for the month. You must email DigitalOcean within two billing cycles, and the credit only applies to future Droplet charges. Scheduled maintenance, your own configuration errors and third-party outages are excluded. ProfileTree's monitoring recorded 99.99%. Treat the SLA as a promise about the platform, not measured uptime for your site.

Is DigitalOcean good for WordPress? Do you need to be a developer?

How do you set up WordPress on a DigitalOcean Droplet?

  1. Create a Droplet from the 1-Click WordPress app. WebPickr and Better Stack both saw a server ready in about 55 seconds.
  2. Pick a data center close to your visitors. DigitalOcean has 16 in 13 cities.
  3. Point your domain at the Droplet, and install an SSL certificate.
  4. Turn on a page cache, and check that it works. Without one, WebPickr saw 1.66 seconds to first byte.
  5. Switch on backups, and add the 20% or 30% to your budget.
  6. Set up updates and security, because the server is yours to look after.

DigitalOcean vs Hetzner and other alternatives: how does it compare?

WebPickr compared its $6 DigitalOcean Droplet in Singapore with a Hetzner server at $9.59. DigitalOcean was cheaper and included twice the traffic. The Hetzner server was much faster at raw computing: a Geekbench single-core score of 1,453 against 462. With a page cache, the two matched at 0.40 and 0.41 seconds. That is the trade-off: DigitalOcean wins on price, ecosystem and documentation, and loses on cheap CPU. Better Stack lists the ecosystem, managed databases and documentation as DigitalOcean's strengths, and lists European audiences and CPU-heavy workloads on a budget as weaker fits. What are the disadvantages of DigitalOcean?

  • You manage the server yourself.
  • Backups cost 20% to 30% extra.
  • WordPress is slow without caching on the cheap Regular CPU.
  • Support by chat costs extra.
  • A powered-off Droplet still bills.

Who should choose DigitalOcean, and who should avoid it?

Choose it if you are a developer or agency, you want predictable prices and strong network speed, and you can run a server.

Avoid it if you want a managed WordPress host, human help in chat or a simple setup.
