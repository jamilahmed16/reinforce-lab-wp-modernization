Flywheel is managed WordPress hosting for designers and agencies, and WP Engine owns it. Plans on its own pricing page start at $25 a month on annual billing for one site. This page covers what Flywheel costs, what each plan includes, how it tested, who it suits and how to set up your first site. Prices were read on Flywheel's own pricing page on 20 November 2026.

Is Flywheel worth it? The short version

  • Plans from $25 a month. Starter is $25 a month on annual billing and $30 on monthly billing, for one site with 25,000 visits.
  • Built for client work. You can hand billing to a client, save a site as a Blueprint and clone a site in one click.
  • Solid in tests. Independent testers saw load times near one second and uptime above 99.9%.
  • Costly for one small site. Shared hosts cost a few dollars a month. You pay for managed care, backups and support.
  • It is WordPress only. Email hosting and domain names are not included.

What is Flywheel WordPress hosting?

Flywheel sells managed hosting for WordPress. "Managed" means Flywheel runs the servers, the caching, the backups and the security for you. Each site runs on its own container and not on a shared server, and Flywheel says every site is on a VPS. The company is owned by WP Engine and runs on Google Cloud. It is also the maker of Local, a free tool that runs WordPress on your own computer. Local is not hosting. You can use it with any host, and it connects to Flywheel for pushing a site live.

Does Flywheel raise its price at renewal?

The pricing page shows one price per plan and does not mention a higher renewal price. VettedSaaS says Flywheel does not raise prices at renewal. That is a third-party claim, so check your order summary. This is a real difference from hosts like Bluehost, where the price jumps after the first term.

Is Flywheel good for hosting? What is included

Every plan lists these features on the pricing page:

  • FlyCache caching that Flywheel tunes for common plugins and themes.
  • A Fastly CDN at no extra cost.
  • A free SSL certificate for every site.
  • Nightly backups kept for 30 days, with one-click restore and snapshots you can name.
  • Staging sites, site cloning and Blueprints.
  • SFTP and an SSH gateway for command-line work.
  • Free migrations, done by Flywheel's team.
  • 24/7 chat support from WordPress specialists.
  • Billing transfer, so a client can own the plan.
  • StudioPress themes and the Genesis Framework at no cost.

How is Flywheel support?

  • Channels: 24/7 live chat, in-app tickets and help docs. No phone line is listed.
  • Migrations: Flywheel moves your sites for free and usually has the copy ready for you to review in 24 to 48 hours.
  • Refunds: Flywheel's home page lists a 60-day money-back guarantee.

Flywheel pros and cons

Pros

  • Billing transfer, Blueprints, staging and cloning built for client work.
  • Nightly backups kept 30 days, with one-click restore.
  • Free migrations and 24/7 chat support.

Cons

Who should use Flywheel, and who should look elsewhere?

Flywheel suits: freelancers and small agencies that host client sites, designers who want to hand billing to a client, and anyone who wants managed WordPress with a simple dashboard.

Look elsewhere if: you run one small blog and want the lowest price, you need email hosting in the same plan, or you want a published uptime guarantee. Our Hostinger review covers a cheaper start.

How to make a website with Flywheel WordPress hosting from scratch

This is the step-by-step guide that this page has always carried. The steps are the same, with current plan names.

Step 1: Buy a domain name

Flywheel does not sell domain names. Buy one from a domain registrar first, or keep the one you already own. You will point it at Flywheel in step 5.

Step 2: Select a hosting plan on Flywheel

Choose Starter for one site. Choose Freelance or Agency if you host client sites. Pick annual billing for two months free, and read the plan name and visit limit in your cart.

Step 3: Create your site on Flywheel

Add a new site in the dashboard, pick a data center near your visitors and set a username and password. If you already have a site, ask Flywheel to migrate it for free.

Step 4: Customize your WordPress site

Log in to WordPress, pick a theme and add your pages. Use a staging site to test changes, and save a Blueprint if you will build similar sites again.

Step 5: Find the DNS record and connect your domain

Copy the DNS records from the site's domain screen in Flywheel. Paste them into your registrar's DNS settings, and wait for the change to spread. This can take a few hours.

Step 6: Add SSL

Turn on the free SSL certificate in the dashboard. Then check that the site loads over https and that the padlock shows.

Final verdict: is Flywheel worth it in 2026?

Yes for freelancers and agencies that host client sites and want managed care, backups and billing transfer in one place. No for a single small site that does not need it. Check the plan, the visit limit and the refund terms before you pay.
