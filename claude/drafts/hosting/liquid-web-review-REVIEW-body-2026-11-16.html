Liquid Web is a US host known for managed WordPress, WooCommerce, VPS and dedicated servers. It is part of Nexcess, so a Nexcess search leads to the same family of products. This Liquid Web review reads Liquid Web's own plan page, five independent test reports and the top-ranking reviews. The pattern is clear. Pages load fast, servers handle 1,000 concurrent users without errors, and support is human. The weak spots are a slow WooCommerce checkout in one test and no data center in Asia. Here are the details, so you can decide quickly.

What did we find? The short version

  • Speed is top tier. Webhostingcat measured a 100% GTmetrix score, a 202 ms time to first byte and a 433 ms Largest Contentful Paint.
  • Load handling is excellent. Two separate tests saw 0% errors at 1,000 concurrent users: 606 ms and 489 ms averages.
  • It is not for far-away visitors. A 3-month test saw first-byte times under 100 ms in the US, UK and Australia and up to 1.9 seconds in India.
  • WooCommerce checkout was slow in one test. Webhostingcat saw a 2.8 second time to first byte at checkout.
  • Liquid Web is part of Nexcess. Nexcess.com is the parent brand for managed cloud, and Liquid Web sells the managed WordPress plans.

Is Liquid Web good for WooCommerce?

It depends on the store. Sayan Samanta says Liquid Web is best for high-traffic WooCommerce stores and membership sites in the US, UK or Australia, and lists 10 PHP workers per site and free Object Cache Pro. Webhostingcat saw a fast home page and product page, with a time to first byte of 61 and 73 ms. Its checkout test was poor: a 2.8 second time to first byte and a 5.5 second full load. It advises that only lighter-traffic, smaller WooCommerce stores should consider Liquid Web. Test your own checkout in the first days. If your store is large, compare Kinsta and Cloudways.

Is Liquid Web reliable?

Liquid Web lists a 99.9999% power and network SLA. SimonHosting recorded 100% uptime on a VPS over several weeks and noted a proactive email about a DNS issue that Liquid Web fixed before it noticed. Website Planet saw no downtime on a Nexcess Spark plan over a week. The tests are short windows, so read them as snapshots.

Which Liquid Web plan should you pick?

  • Pro WordPress ($19 for 50,000 visits): one business site that needs daily backups kept 14 days, a staging site and the Edge CDN.
  • Elite WordPress ($29 for 50,000 visits): a site where you want 30 days of backups, more storage and EdgePress on top of the CDN.
  • Higher visit tiers: choose the tier that matches your monthly visits, up to 12.5 million. Check overage rules before a big campaign.
  • Managed VPS or bare metal: a developer-run server, with a different dashboard and setup. SimonHosting says the first VPS WordPress setup took over an hour and needed support.

How do you move a site to Liquid Web?

  1. Pick a plan and a data center close to your visitors: the US, the UK or Australia.
  2. Ask for the human-assisted migration listed on the plan page.
  3. Test the copy on a staging address: pages, forms, checkout and speed. Check your checkout carefully, because Webhostingcat saw a slow one.
  4. Point your domain at Liquid Web, and keep the old host live for a few days.
  5. Watch rankings, errors and speed for two weeks.

What are the disadvantages of Liquid Web?

  • No data centers in Asia, so visitors there see slow first-byte times.
  • WooCommerce checkout was slow in one test.
  • The page we read shows no money-back guarantee.
  • Plans are priced by visits, and prices rise with traffic.
  • A Cloudflare Enterprise setup problem in one reviewer's test.
  • Webhostingcat says beginners can find a better fit.

Who should choose Liquid Web, and who should avoid it?

Choose it if you run a business site, a WordPress agency site or a WooCommerce store with US, UK or Australian visitors, and you want managed care.

Avoid it if most of your visitors are in Asia, you are a beginner who wants a simple shared host, or you want a money-back guarantee stated on the page.
