DreamHost is an independently owned host with a clear price list: it shows the renewal price next to the first-term price. The cheapest plan hosts 25 websites. The catch is that the low price needs a long term, and email is free for only three months. This page puts the prices, the limits, the test results and the WordPress setup in one place. Prices were read on DreamHost's own pages on 20 November 2026.

Is DreamHost worth it? The short version

  • The term decides the price. Launch is $2.89 a month on a 1-year term and renews at $10.99. On a 4-year term it is $1.99 and renews at $7.99.
  • Lots of sites for the money. Launch allows 25 websites with 25 GB of NVMe storage and daily backups.
  • Speed is average. In WPBeginner's July 2026 tests DreamHost loaded in 986 ms, and Hostinger in 659 ms.
  • No phone support. Support is 24/7 chat and tickets.
  • The refund window is 30 days. Older reviews that say 97 days describe plans DreamHost no longer sells.

What do you get with DreamHost?

  • Daily automated backups on all three plans, a free SSL certificate, unmetered bandwidth and a free domain for the first year.
  • An AI website builder, called Liftoff, and on-demand performance scans.
  • A 100% uptime guarantee. If DreamHost misses it, you get one day of hosting credit for each hour of downtime, up to 10% of your next renewal.
  • A custom control panel, not cPanel.

Some features are missing on the shared plans. DreamHost says you need DreamPress for the CDN and one-click staging. Email is free for the first 3 months only. After that it costs from $1.67 a month for one mailbox on annual billing, or from $1.99 on monthly billing, according to WPRoom.

DreamHost nonprofit hosting: is it still free?

Not confirmed. DreamHost has long offered one free shared hosting plan to US 501(c)(3) and 501(c)(19) nonprofits, and a TechEMPOWER summary of DreamHost's help article (checked May 2026) still lists it. But a research study dated 3 October 2026 found that DreamHost's own nonprofit-discount page returns a 404 error, and several 2026 reports say the program closed to new sign-ups on 31 March 2026 while older accounts are still honored. We could not confirm either view on DreamHost's own site. Do not plan around a free plan. If you are a nonprofit, ask DreamHost support for the current terms before you apply. If the program is gone, the standard Launch plan is the fallback.

What is DreamPress, and do you need it?

DreamPress is DreamHost's managed WordPress plan. DreamPress 1 was $14.99 a month in the first year and renews at $19.99. It covers 1 website, 15 GB of NVMe storage, 40,000 monthly visits and 14 days of backups. It adds the CDN, staging and the managed features. Launch hosts 25 sites for far less, so DreamPress only makes sense for one important site that needs those extras. WPSchool measured DreamPress at 268 ms time to first byte against 487 ms for shared hosting on its older test plan.

Is DreamHost good for WordPress?

Yes for blogs, small business sites and several small sites on one plan. The shared plans give you many sites, NVMe storage and daily backups. WPSchool installed WordPress with WooCommerce and seven plugins on its test site and ran it for 30 days. The limits are no staging or CDN on shared plans, and a first response that trails LiteSpeed hosts. How is DreamHost support?

  • Channels: 24/7 live chat and tickets. There is no phone line. WPRoom says a one-time callback costs $9.95.
  • Wait times: HostingAdvice reported live chat waits of about 30 minutes in its hands-on test.
  • Languages: WPRoom lists English and Spanish.

Where are DreamHost's data centers?

In late October 2026, WPThinker saw data centers in Oregon, Virginia and Amsterdam. An April 2026 review said the US only, so the list may have grown. If most of your visitors are in Asia or Australia, a host with servers closer to them may be quicker.

Is the DreamHost 97-day guarantee still real?

No, not for new plans. Older reviews praise a 97-day money-back guarantee. DreamHost's current pricing page says 30 days. RankPlot reported in June 2026 that DreamHost retired the older Shared Starter and Shared Unlimited plans for new buyers. Plan for 30 days.

Who should use DreamHost, and who should look elsewhere?

DreamHost suits: bloggers and small businesses with several small sites, freelancers with client sites, eligible US nonprofits, and anyone who wants the renewal price shown up front.

Look elsewhere if: you need phone support, you want staging and a CDN on a budget plan, speed is your top priority, or most of your visitors are far from the US and Europe. Our best cloud hosting page covers managed cloud servers.

How do you build a WordPress site on DreamHost? Step by step

  1. Choose a plan and a term. Launch covers 25 sites. A 4-year term has the lowest renewal.
  2. Register your free domain. It is free for the first year. Check what it costs to renew.
  3. Create your account and check the cart. Note the email trial, which becomes a paid plan after 3 months.
  4. Install WordPress. Use DreamHost's panel to add WordPress to your domain. If you have a site elsewhere, ask about the free WordPress migration.
  5. Add caching. Shared plans have no server-level cache, so install a caching plugin.
  6. Pick a theme and add pages. Add a home page, an about page and a contact page first. Then set up SEO and security plugins.
  7. Check backups and set a renewal reminder. Backups are daily. Add the renewal date and the new price to your calendar.

Which DreamHost plan should you pick?

  • A blog or a few small sites: Launch.
  • More sites and more traffic: Growth.
  • A busy agency or store: Scale.
  • One important site that needs staging and a CDN: DreamPress.

Final verdict: is DreamHost worth it in 2026?

Yes, if you want many small sites on one cheap plan and you will commit to a long term. Pick the 4-year term only if you are sure, budget for email after three months, and use the 30-day window to test it. Skip it if you need phone support or the fastest speed.
