hosting.com is the new name of A2 Hosting. Hostinger is one of the most searched low-cost hosts. Both advertise very low sale prices, and both have long lists of AI tools. This page compares what each one says on its own pages, and what independent testers found, as of 15 November 2026. We did not test either host ourselves.

The short version: hosting.com has the lower sale price and the shorter commitment. Hostinger has the lower renewal price. The better deal depends on whether you plan to stay after the first term.

What did A2 Hosting become?

In April 2025, A2 Hosting announced on its own blog that it was now hosting.com. If you read old A2 Hosting reviews, they describe the same company. Prices, plan names and features have changed since those reviews, so use pages from this year.

Over the first year, hosting.com Starter costs $17.88. Hostinger asks for $95.52 up front for 48 months, which works out to about $23.88 a year. After the first term, Hostinger's renewal is $3 a month lower. If you will stay for four years or more, Hostinger's longer commitment locks a low price for longer. If you want to leave after a year, hosting.com asks for less money and less time.

The plans are not the same size. Hostinger Premium lists 3 sites and 20 GB of SSD storage. We did not find a matching storage figure for hosting.com Starter on the page we read, so check the plan details before you compare the two on price alone.

What do the independent tests say?

The two hosts were tested by different publishers with different methods, so the numbers do not line up. Read them as separate checks, not a race.

  • Hostinger: All About Cookies (updated 9 November 2026) tested a paid plan on a live site, with speed tests from three locations and 9 days of uptime monitoring. It reported 100% uptime and an average response time of 138 ms. It also noted that Hostinger is not PCI compliant.
  • hosting.com: HostingAdvice (updated 12 November 2026) tested for 2 weeks with GTmetrix and live chat. It reported a largest contentful paint of 579 ms on the cheap plan and live chat replies in seconds. It lists hosting.com's renewal prices as among the highest it saw.

Both testers point to the same weak spot on the price side. Hostinger's renewals and hosting.com's renewals are higher than the sale price. That is the cost to plan for.

What AI features does each host list?

Both hosts list AI tools on their own pages. We have not tested them, so treat them as claims.

  • Hostinger lists an AI website builder, AI credits that vary by plan, an AI assistant called Hostinger Agent and an MCP server for managing a VPS from an AI tool.
  • hosting.com lists an AI assistant called Orbi, an AI website builder for WordPress, an MCP server, and a Claude Skills Pack on Pro and Max.

The lists are close. If AI tools decide your choice, try each host's free options or the money-back period before you commit to a long term.

Who should pick which?

Pick hosting.com if you want the lowest first-year bill and a short commitment. Pick Hostinger if you will stay for years and want the lower renewal price. If you cannot decide, compare the entry plan prices again on the day you buy. Both offers change.
