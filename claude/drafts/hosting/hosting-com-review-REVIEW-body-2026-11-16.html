hosting.com is the new name of A2 Hosting. It has some of the lowest first-year prices on the market, a long list of AI tools and fast servers. It also has some of the highest renewal prices, and one major reviewer calls it a poor fit for beginners. This hosting.com review uses hosting.com's own pages, three independent reviews with test results and the top-ranking review pages. It puts the findings side by side, so you can see what you pay, what you get and who should skip it.

  • Speed is good. HostingAdvice measured a 579 ms Largest Contentful Paint on the cheap plan. CNET found fast servers.
  • It is not for beginners. CNET calls setup overly complicated and support poor. Website Planet says support lacks product knowledge.
  • AI tools are a strong point. hosting.com lists an AI assistant, an AI website builder, an MCP server and a Claude Skills Pack.

What do hosting.com plans include: backups, staging and security?

  • Backups: daily backups are listed on every shared plan on hosting.com's own page. Website Planet found no manual backup button in the custom control panel, so check how restores work.
  • Staging: staging sites and preview URLs come with Pro and Max, and Website Planet found one-click staging and cloning.
  • Security: DDoS protection, brute force defense and pre-installed SSL on shared plans. Website Planet notes that some hosts charge extra for this.
  • Pre-installed plugins: 11 plugins came with the test site, including the free version of Jetpack, AIOSEO, WPForms and MonsterInsights.
  • Email: emails and security are included on Starter, and Max lists 40 mailboxes.
  • WordPress: preinstalled on all WordPress plans, and your DNS details show on the first dashboard screen.

Where are the hosting.com data centers?

Website Planet lists two in the US and one each in Canada, Mexico, the UK, Germany, India, Singapore and Australia. WordPress plans offer only four choices: Dallas, London, Frankfurt and Singapore. Pick the location closest to your visitors, because Website Planet saw slower speeds in Singapore and Australia. The checkout offers monthly, annual, two-year and three-year terms. You need a year or more to get the free domain.

What is hosting.com support like? Is there phone support?

Website Planet says hosting.com offers pre-sale support by phone, live chat and a knowledge base. After you buy, you also get a ticket system. Support is in English only. Its experience was mixed: the live chat agent was confused, and the phone agent was more proficient but slower. HostingAdvice found live chat replies in seconds. CNET rated support as poor. If support matters, test it in the first 30 days. Max plans list priority support.

How accurate is the hosting.com website?

Website Planet found that some plan descriptions were incomplete or wrong. For example, the site advertises East and West Coast US data centers, but WordPress plans use only Dallas. It also described Performance Plus as an option when it is standard, and it mentioned a plan that does not exist. Plan names, such as Starter, Plus, Pro and Max, repeat across hosting types, which makes choosing harder. Website Planet advises checking plan features twice before you sign up. We read the plan pages on 15 November, and our blockers list below flags what still needs a check.

Is hosting.com good for beginners?

Probably not. CNET says it recommends hosting.com only for experienced website owners or people comfortable learning new tech. It lists the setup as overly complicated and support as poor. Website Planet says its custom control panel looks like the WordPress dashboard, opens in its own window and takes time to get used to. Support told Website Planet you may lose your WordPress site if you replace the custom panel with cPanel. HostingAdvice, on the other hand, rated live chat as fast. The reviews differ, so test support early in your 30-day window.

Who should consider hosting.com, and who should avoid it?

Consider it if you want the lowest first-year bill, you are comfortable with hosting tools and you want AI features included. It also suits people who plan to move again after the first term.

Avoid it if you are new to hosting, you want a low price for years, or you want a simple setup. Our Hostinger review covers a lower-cost option with a longer sale term.
