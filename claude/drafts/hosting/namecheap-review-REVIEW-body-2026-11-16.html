Namecheap is best known for cheap domains. Its shared hosting follows the same plan: very low promo prices, a 30-day free period and a long list of included extras. This Namecheap hosting review reads Namecheap's own plan page, six independent test reports with real numbers and the top-ranking reviews. The pattern is clear. Pages load fast for visitors near the US, slow for visitors far away, and the renewal price is about double the promo price. Here are the details, so you can decide quickly.

What did we find? The short version

  • The promo price is low. Stellar starts at $2.28 a month, and you can try shared hosting free for 30 days.
  • Renewal is about double. Stellar renews at $55.88 a year, which is 2 to 2.4 times the promo price depending on the plan.
  • Speed depends on where your visitors are. Testers measured fast responses from the US and 1 second or more in Asia, unless a CDN is on.
  • Uptime is good, not perfect. Namecheap claims 100%. Independent tests measured between 99.29% and 100%.
  • It suits small sites. Cybernews calls it a great domain provider and weak web hosting.

What does Namecheap hosting include?

Namecheap shared hosting has three plans: Stellar, Stellar Plus and Stellar Business. All three list unmetered bandwidth, free automatic SSL and a Supersonic CDN for domains that use Namecheap's hosting nameservers. Namecheap also gives 50 free Standard SSL certificates for the first year, according to its plan page, and lists Imunify360 security and free website migration on every plan. Data centers are in the US, the EU, Singapore and the UK. HostingGuider notes a one-click WordPress installer through Softaculous and 24/7 live chat with no phone line confirmed. It also reports that a free domain is not included, and that it could not verify green hosting.

Is Namecheap reliable? Does the 100% uptime guarantee mean anything?

Namecheap advertises a 100% uptime guarantee. That is a claim, not a result. HostingTestLab reads the terms this way: you get one free day of hosting for each hour of unplanned downtime, up to one month, if you open a billing ticket within 10 calendar days, and Namecheap decides whether an outage qualifies. Measured uptime across the sources we read ran from 99.29% (Cybernews, two weeks) to 99.95% (TopSiteHosters, 30 days) and 99.99% (Kripesh Adwani, one year, 34 minutes of downtime). HostingTestLab also reports that an August 2026 failure at Namecheap's Phoenix data center took hosting down for about a day. We found that in one source only, so check Namecheap's status history before you rely on it.

Which Namecheap plan should you pick?

  • Stellar: one small site or up to 3 sites. It has 20 GB of SSD and backups twice a week. It is the plan Cyber Naira tested, and it found the resources held up for a single site with modest traffic.

How do you create a WordPress website on Namecheap?

  1. Choose a plan and a data center close to your visitors.
  2. Register a domain, or connect one you own to Namecheap's hosting nameservers.

What are the disadvantages of Namecheap?

  • Renewal is about 2 to 2.4 times the promo price.
  • Speed drops for visitors far from the US unless you add a CDN.
  • Uptime measured below the 100% claim in several tests.
  • Limited malware protection, according to Cybernews, even though Namecheap lists Imunify360.

Who should buy Namecheap hosting, and who should avoid it?

Buy it if you run a small site, a portfolio or a first blog, most of your visitors are in North America or Europe, and you want domains and hosting in one account.

Avoid it if your audience is mostly in Asia or South America and you will not use a CDN, you need strong security tools, or you want phone support. Cybernews' view fits: a great domain provider and weak web hosting.
