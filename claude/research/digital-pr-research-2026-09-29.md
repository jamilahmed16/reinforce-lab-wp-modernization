## B. Google (primary)
- Link spam examples include "links with optimized anchor text in articles, guest posts, or press releases distributed on other sites", large-scale guest-posting campaigns with keyword-rich anchors, and buying/selling links for ranking. Paid links are fine when qualified `nofollow`/`sponsored`. — https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies
- `sponsored` for ads/paid placements (nofollow acceptable, sponsored preferred). — https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/qualify-outbound-links
- Links are used for relevance and discovery; crawlable `<a href>`; anchor text descriptive, concise, relevant; avoid keyword stuffing. — https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable
- SEO Starter Guide: Google finds pages mainly through links; promote content; over-promotion can look manipulative. — https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide
- Wire-service/press-release sites are not automatically site reputation abuse. — spam policies.

## C. Cision 2026 State of the Media (VERIFIED from the PDF; 1,899 respondents, 19 markets, Jan–Feb 2026)
- 79% most likely to engage with pitches relevant to their beat/audience; 35% timely angle; 33% credible data or research.
- 66% rely on PR-provided content (releases, pitches, media kits) for story ideas.
- Want more of: 47% data or research; 45% embargoed/early access; 42% access to experts.
- 97% prefer pitches by email. 64% say follow up once. 52% expect a reply within 24 hours.
- Rejection reasons: 82% not relevant; 53% too promotional; 34% no clear news angle.
- Source: https://www.prnewswire.com/content/dam/prnewswire/resources/white-papers/Cision_2026_State_of_the_Media_Report.pdf
