Short answer

Pharma SEO works when it answers the questions clinicians, patients and partners ask, with content that is accurate, sourced and reviewed, and that never promotes prescription medicines where the rules forbid it. Unbranded disease education and corporate content carry most of the search value.

Most pharma websites are built for compliance first and search second. That keeps them safe, but it also leaves the questions people ask Google and AI tools to be answered by forums, news sites and competitors.

Sector rules

Rules to know before you publish

Check every page against these before it goes live. Your own regulatory team has the final word.

Rule and what it means for searchCompliance
R1

No promotion of prescription medicines to the publicUK and EU

Patient-facing pages can educate about conditions, but cannot promote a prescription medicine. Keep product detail behind a healthcare professional gate.

R2

Fair balance in the USUS

Where product claims are allowed, risk information must sit alongside benefits, including in snippets you write for search.

R3

Health content is held to a higher standardGoogle, all markets

Show who wrote and reviewed each page, their qualifications, and the sources behind every claim.

General guidance, not legal or regulatory advice.

What works and what to avoid

Works

  • Plain-language disease pages with named reviewers
  • Citing guidelines and published studies
  • Separate areas for patients and professionals

Avoid

  • Product names in patient-facing titles where promotion is restricted
  • Unsourced claims about outcomes
  • Publishing before medical and legal review

Questions

FAQs

Can pharma companies do SEO at all?

Yes. Corporate, pipeline and unbranded disease education content can all be optimised. Product promotion rules decide what you can say and to whom.

Who should review pharma content?

A qualified medical reviewer for accuracy, and your regulatory or legal team for compliance, before anything is published.
